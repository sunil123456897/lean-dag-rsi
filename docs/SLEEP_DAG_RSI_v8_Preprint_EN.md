## 1. Introduction

Recent progress in automated scientific discovery has been driven by coupling large language models (LLMs) with executable evaluators in evolutionary loops, including *FunSearch* [Romera-Paredes et al., 2024], *EoH (Evolution of Heuristics)* [Liu et al., 2024], *ReEvo* [Ye et al., 2024], *LLM-SR* [Shojaee et al., 2025], *AlphaEvolve* [Novikov et al., 2025], *AlgoTune* [Press et al., 2025], large-scale mathematical exploration [Georgiev et al., 2025; Khrulkov et al., 2026], *ThetaEvolve* [Wang et al., 2025], *TTT-Discover* [Yuksekgonul et al., 2026], *Dream-RSI* [Li et al., 2026], and *DiscoveryRSI* [Zhang et al., 2026]. In parallel, continual parametric learning architectures such as *SLEEP* [Behrouz et al., 2026], *HOPE / Continuum Memory System (CMS)* [Behrouz et al., 2025], and *Gated DeltaNet* [Yang et al., 2025] have sought to endow neural networks with online fast-weight adaptation during "Wake" cycles and offline slow-weight consolidation during "Sleep" cycles.

Despite their conceptual appeal, deploying these systems on long-horizon discovery tasks reveals a fundamental disconnect between high-level algorithmic descriptions and the underlying optimization geometry:

1. **Pathological Replay & Credit Objectives:** As we prove in Section 2, the counterfactual replay objective in Equation (1) of *Dream-RSI* [Li et al., 2026] and the hypothesis credit weighting in Equation (17) of *DiscoveryRSI* [Zhang et al., 2026] contain incentive inversions that actively reward dummy batch inflation and risk-averse syntax repairs over genuine structural crossovers.
2. **Tree Isolation & Textual Blindness to Ill-Conditioned Manifolds:** Strictly tree-structured search graphs prevent recombining orthogonal breakthroughs discovered in sibling branches. Furthermore, when a discrete code structure depends on continuous parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ lying in a rotated epistatic valley ($\kappa(G) \ge 10^8$) or a non-smooth minimax crease ($\min_{1 \le m \le M} A_m(\boldsymbol{\theta})$), random LLM text edits and naive first-order solvers stall completely.
3. **Black-Box Drift & Dead-End Contamination in Neural Sleep:** Standard parametric consolidation (*Vanilla SLEEP*) indiscriminately distills all wake trajectories into slow Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) weights, imprinting rejected dead-ends into long-term memory, while unnormalized fast associative memories (*Hebbian / Delta-Rule*) diverge quadratically ($\propto \|x_t\|_2^2$) under heavy-tailed sensor spikes. Conversely, pure DAG-based code masks (`loss_mask = 0` on failures) waste $100\%$ of compute whenever an exploratory round yields zero passing candidates.

## 2. Mathematical Audit of Baseline RSI Architectures & `Lean-DAG-RSI v1–v4`

### 2.1. Proposition 1: Probe-Spam Incentive Inversion in *Dream-RSI* Equation (1)

In Section 3 (p. 6, Eq. (1)) of *Dream-RSI* [Li et al., 2026], the counterfactual replay value of exploration policy $m$ on historical discovery tree $\mathcal{T}_i$ is defined as:
$$V_i^m = \max_{v \in \mathcal{T}_i^{m, < k_i^{m,\star}}} s_v - \beta_1 N_i^m + \beta_2 \frac{N_i^m}{\max\{1, k_i^{m,\star}\}}, \tag{1}$$
where $s_v$ is the verifier score of node $v$, $N_i^m$ is the total number of non-root nodes opened (verifier probes consumed), $k_i^{m,\star} \ge 1$ is the number of sequential decision rounds until termination, and $\beta_1, \beta_2 \ge 0$ are scalar weights penalizing total probes and rewarding average batch parallelism $N_i^m / k_i^{m,\star}$.

> **Proposition 1 (Positive Gradient for Wasted Parallel Probes).** *Let $\beta_2 > \beta_1 > 0$ (e.g., standard weights $\beta_1 = 0.01, \beta_2 = 0.05$). For any replay trajectory terminating in $k_i^{m,\star} < \beta_2 / \beta_1$ sequential rounds, the partial derivative of $V_i^m$ in Eq. (1) with respect to the number of consumed verifier probes $N_i^m$ at fixed best score $\max_v s_v$ is strictly positive:*
> $$\frac{\partial V_i^m}{\partial N_i^m} = -\beta_1 + \frac{\beta_2}{k_i^{m,\star}} > 0 \qquad \forall\, k_i^{m,\star} \in \left\{1, \dots, \left\lceil \frac{\beta_2}{\beta_1} \right\rceil - 1\right\}. \tag{2}$$
> *Proof.* Differentiating Eq. (1) with respect to $N_i^m$ while holding $\max_v s_v$ and $k_i^{m,\star} \ge 1$ constant yields $\partial V_i^m / \partial N_i^m = -\beta_1 + \beta_2 / k_i^{m,\star}$. Whenever $k_i^{m,\star} < \beta_2 / \beta_1$, we have $\beta_2 / k_i^{m,\star} > \beta_1$. In particular, at $k_i^{m,\star} = 1$ with $\beta_1 = 0.01, \beta_2 = 0.05$, appending $\Delta N$ completely useless dummy probes with zero score improvement ($\Delta \max s_v = 0$) strictly increases the objective by $+0.04\,\Delta N > 0$. $\square$

Furthermore, forensic inspection of *Dream-RSI* Appendix B.2 (`Listing 2`, `evaluate_policy`) reveals that the reference code does not implement Eq. (1) at all, optimizing instead `pareto.auc - lambda * (1.0 / mean_parallelism)`, which similarly penalizes narrow batches and encourages redundant parallel probes.

**Resolution in `Lean-DAG-RSI` (`engine.py:726`):** We replace Eq. (1) with a strictly monotone replay objective:
$$V_{\text{Lean}}(\mathcal{T}, \pi) = \max_{v \in \tau(\pi)} s_v - \beta_1 N_{\text{probes}}(\pi) - \beta_2 k_{\text{rounds}}(\pi), \qquad \frac{\partial V_{\text{Lean}}}{\partial N_{\text{probes}}} = -\beta_1 < 0, \quad \frac{\partial V_{\text{Lean}}}{\partial k_{\text{rounds}}} = -\beta_2 < 0. \tag{3}$$
Because replay enforces strict **prefix-only observability** (`Suite 4`: unrevealed child scores $s_v$ are masked until probed) and scale-normalizes the penalties as $\beta_1 = \tilde{\beta}_1 \cdot \widehat{\operatorname{IQR}}(\Delta^+), \beta_2 = \tilde{\beta}_2 \cdot \widehat{\operatorname{IQR}}(\Delta^+)$ ($\tilde{\beta}_1 = 0.01, \tilde{\beta}_2 = 0.05$), Eq. (3) never induces ex-post width-1 degenerate policy bias: a width-1 policy incurs a $k_{\text{rounds}}$ penalty for every single probe and misses parallel sibling breakthroughs, whereas $V_{\text{Lean}}$ rewards parallel exploration that genuinely increases $\max_{v \in \tau(\pi)} s_v$ while strictly penalizing zero-gain padding probes.

### 2.2. Overcoming Support Censorship via Shift-Invariant Damped Tukey Continuation (`s_cf`)

When an alternative policy $\pi'$ replays a fixed historical tree $\mathcal{T}$ and chooses to deepen a rapidly improving branch node $u$ ($\Delta_u = s(u) - s(\text{parent}(u)) > 0$) that was prematurely halted at depth $d_u < D_{\max}$ in the recorded log, standard replay returns a no-op ($\Delta_{\text{next}} = 0$). This *Support Censorship* systematically penalizes policies that prioritize high-slope under-explored branches.

In `LeanDAGWorld` (`engine.py:678`), probing an unexpanded frontier node $u$ with $\Delta_u > 0$ spawns up to $m \in \{1, 2\}$ virtual counterfactual continuation steps with damped cumulative Tukey fencing ($\gamma = 0.55$ reflecting concave diminishing marginal returns along iterative local refinement branches):
$$s_{\text{cf}}(u, m) = s(u) + \left(\sum_{j=1}^m \gamma^j\right) \min\bigl(\Delta_u, C_{\text{Tukey}}(\Delta^+; \Delta_u)\bigr), \qquad \gamma = 0.55, \tag{4}$$
$$C_{\text{Tukey}}(\Delta^+; \Delta_u) = (1 - \alpha_{\text{cf}})\,C_{\text{comp}}(\Delta_u) + \alpha_{\text{cf}}\bigl(Q_{75}(\Delta^+) + 1.5\,\operatorname{IQR}(\Delta^+)\bigr), \qquad C_{\text{comp}}(\Delta_u) = \frac{\Delta_u}{1 + \Delta_u / S_\Delta}, \tag{4a}$$
where $\alpha_{\text{cf}} = \min\bigl(1,\, \max(0, (|\Delta^+| - 1)/3)\bigr) \in [0, 1]$ smoothly interpolates between the small-sample compression barrier ($|\Delta^+| = 1 \implies \alpha_{\text{cf}} = 0$) and the empirical interquartile fence ($|\Delta^+| \ge 4 \implies \alpha_{\text{cf}} = 1$), eliminating any step discontinuity at $|\Delta^+| = 4$. Crucially, the scale factor $S_\Delta = \max\bigl(\operatorname{mean}(\Delta^+),\; |s(u) - s(\text{root})|,\; 10^{-6}\bigr)$ depends solely on score differences rather than raw score magnitude $|s(u)|$, guaranteeing exact **shift invariance** $s_{\text{cf}}(u, m; s + c) = s_{\text{cf}}(u, m; s) + c$ for any constant offset $c \in \mathbb{R}$ across all sample sizes $|\Delta^+| \ge 1$ (`Suite 19h`). Across our 50-task synthetic benchmark (`lean_dag_rsi_benchmark.py`), `V_Lean + s_cf` defeats *Dream-RSI* `OptimalPolicy` by **37 : 2** (11 ties) while reducing verifier calls by **30.6%**.

### 2.3. Error-Fingerprint DAG Crossover & Anchored Multi-Parent Hyper-Crossover (`v2–v4`)

To break tree isolation without triggering combinatorial junk merges, every evaluated node $u$ records a binary pass mask $\mathbf{m}(u) \in \{0, 1\}^M$ (`pass_mask`) and continuous normalized bucket margins $\boldsymbol{\phi}(u) \in [0, 1]^M$ (`bucket_margins`) across $M$ diagnostic regime slices, along with the set of modified AST symbols $\mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(u)$ (`ast_touches` and line hotspots from nanosecond profiler `profile_callable_lines`).

Given the top-scoring anchor parent $u^\star = \arg\max_{u \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{succ}}} \bar{\phi}(u)$ and any candidate parent subset $S \supseteq \{u^\star\}$ ($|S| \ge 2$), `compute_hyper_crossover` (`engine.py:2715`) initializes greedy selection at the singleton anchor seed $S_0 = \{u^\star\}$ (where the pairwise collision penalty is $0$ for $|S| = 1$, and all candidate subsets satisfy $S \supseteq \{u^\star\}$ so AST collisions with $u^\star$ are accounted for from the very first greedy step) and evaluates the **Anchored Excess Margin & Structural Diversity Objective**:
$$\Delta_{\text{Hyper}}(S \mid u^\star) = \underbrace{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M \max_{u \in S} \phi_j(u) - \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M \phi_j(u^\star)}_{\text{Monotone submodular coverage gain } f_{\text{cov}}(S) - f_{\text{cov}}(\{u^\star\})} - \underbrace{\frac{\lambda_{\text{coll}}}{\max\!\bigl(1, \binom{|S|}{2}\bigr)} \sum_{\{u, v\} \subseteq S} \operatorname{IoU}_{\text{AST}}\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(u), \mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(v)\bigr)}_{\text{Scale-invariant AST collision regularizer } P_{\text{AST}}(S) \in [0, \lambda_{\text{coll}}]}. \tag{5}$$
Anchoring the reference term at the fixed seed $u^\star \in S$ ensures that the coverage functional $g_{\text{cov}}(S) = f_{\text{cov}}(S) - f_{\text{cov}}(\{u^\star\}) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M \max_{u \in S}\phi_j(u) - \bar{\phi}(u^\star)$ is **strictly monotone submodular** on all subsets $S \supseteq \{u^\star\}$ (whereas subtracting a moving baseline $\max_{u \in S}\bar{\phi}(u)$ would violate diminishing returns whenever a newly added node shifts the set maximum). In contrast, the pair-normalized AST collision term $P_{\text{AST}}(S) \in [0, \lambda_{\text{coll}}]$ acts as a bounded, scale-invariant structural diversity regularizer across subset sizes $|S|$ (noting that if one normalizes by a fixed cardinality cap $\binom{K_{\max}}{2}$ instead of $\binom{|S|}{2}$, $-P_{\text{AST}}(S)$ is supermodular on subsets, whereas $\binom{|S|}{2}$ keeps the penalty scale-invariant in $[0, \lambda_{\text{coll}}]$ for every $|S| \ge 2$). Greedy selection adds a parent $w \notin S$ if and only if its marginal gain $\Delta_{\text{Hyper}}(S \cup \{w\} \mid u^\star) - \Delta_{\text{Hyper}}(S \mid u^\star)$ exceeds $\varepsilon_{\text{tol}} > 0$ with mean pairwise $\operatorname{IoU}_{\text{AST}} < 0.5$.

### 2.4. Recursive Reduced-Space Invariant (`RRSI`) Pruner & Scale-Invariant Gauss-Newton Epistatic Pruner (`v3–v4`)

When numerical solvers polish tunable continuous parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in [\mathbf{l}, \mathbf{u}] \subset \mathbb{R}^d$, over-parameterized models overfit training noise or drift along coupled epistatic ridges (e.g., $g(\theta_0, \theta_1) = \theta_0 / \theta_1$). We apply a two-stage regularizer:

1. **Recursive Reduced-Space Invariant (`RRSI`) Single-Coordinate Pruner (`prune_tunable_params`, `engine.py:1609`):** Greedily resets passenger coordinates $\theta_i^\star \to \theta_i^{(0)}$ whenever the cumulative drop from the unpruned optimum satisfies $f(\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star) - f(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{try}}) \le \delta_{\text{tol}}$.
2. **Scale-Invariant Epistatic Gauss-Newton Pruner (`prune_epistatic_gauss_newton`, `engine.py:2989`):** Maps $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ to the dimensionless unit hypercube $\tilde{\theta}_i = (\theta_i - l_i)/(u_i - l_i) \in [0, 1]^d$ and evaluates smooth per-bucket margins $\phi_j(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}})$ on a central finite-difference macro-stencil $\delta = 0.04$ (or $\delta = 10^{-3}$ near smooth optima) for tunable subspaces $d \le d_{\max} = 16$ (requiring $1 + 2d + 2d(d-1) \le 481$ lightweight margin evaluations, $< 5\text{ ms}$). ### 4.1. `WaldMicroSentinel`: Strictly Budgeted 5% Multi-Fidelity Sequential UCB95 Early-Veto with Locked Stochastic Trimming

Evaluating doomed candidate mutations across fine grids ($P = 20,256$) or large geometric constraint sets ($\binom{16}{3} = 560$ triangles) wastes $80\text{--}95\%$ of compute. 2. **Disjoint Stratified Anchor ($S_{\text{strat}}$, $S_{\text{adv}} \cap S_{\text{strat}} = \emptyset$):** Uniformly spaced indices sampled without replacement from the complement of $S_{\text{adv}} \cup S_{\text{trim}}$, guaranteeing zero inter-stratum overlap and zero inter-stratum covariance.
3. **Sequential Upper-Confidence-Bound ($\operatorname{UCB}_{95}$) Early-Veto Test with Equal-Weight Risk-Averse Composite & Unbiased Stratified SE:** On the active evaluation slice $S_{\text{adv}} \cup S_{\text{strat}}$ ($k = |S_{\text{adv}}| + |S_{\text{strat}}|$, activated for domain sizes $N \ge N_{\min} = 80$ so $|S_{\text{adv}}|, |S_{\text{strat}}| \ge 2$), let $\Delta_i = s_v(i) - s_u(i)$ denote the pointwise score gain of candidate $v$ over parent $u$ (higher is better), with stratum means $\hat{\mu}_{\text{adv}}, \hat{\mu}_{\text{strat}}$ and within-stratum sample variances $s_{\text{adv}}^2, s_{\text{strat}}^2$.    $$\operatorname{UCB}_{95}(\Delta) = \hat{\mu}_\Delta + \gamma_{\text{UCB}}(k)\,\operatorname{SE}_{\text{strat}}(\Delta) < -\epsilon_{\text{tol}}, \qquad \operatorname{SE}_{\text{strat}}(\Delta) = \sqrt{w_{\text{adv}}^2 \frac{s_{\text{adv}}^2}{|S_{\text{adv}}|} + w_{\text{strat}}^2 \frac{s_{\text{strat}}^2}{|S_{\text{strat}}|}}, \tag{10}$$
   where $w_{\text{adv}} = w_{\text{strat}} = 1/2$ and $\gamma_{\text{UCB}}(k) = 1.96 \sqrt{\frac{k}{k - 1.5}}$ for small samples $2 \le k < 30$ ($\gamma_{\text{UCB}} = 1.96$ for $k \ge 30$, and $\gamma_{\text{UCB}}(28) \approx 2.01, \gamma_{\text{UCB}}(14) \approx 2.08$ at Heilbronn $n=16$). ### 4.2. Proposition 3: Dual Gram Spectral Equivalence, Numerical-Rank $\kappa_{\text{eff}}(G)$ & `SpectralMetaAtlas` Dispatch in `SpectralFrameLifter`

When optimizing $d$ parameters across $M$ regime buckets with Jacobian $J \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d}$ and primal Gauss-Newton matrix $G = \frac{1}{M}J^\top J \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, two classical pathologies arise when $M < d$ (e.g., $M = 6$ buckets and $d = 128$ or $2,560$ parameters):

1. **Rank-Deficient False Alarms:** Because $\operatorname{rank}(G) \le \min(M, d) < d$, the smallest $d - M$ eigenvalues of $G$ are identically zero ($\lambda_{\min}(G) = 0$), causing the naive condition number $\kappa(G) = \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min} \to \infty$ even on perfectly isotropic objectives!
2. **Cubic $\mathcal{O}(d^3)$ Diagonalization Bottleneck:** Forming and diagonalizing $G \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ requires $\mathcal{O}(M d^2 + d^3)$ operations.

> **Proposition 3 (Dual Gram Eigendecomposition & Relative-Threshold Condition Number).** *Let $J \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d}$ with $M < d$, and define the $M \times M$ Dual Gram matrix $K = \frac{1}{M} J J^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times M}$ (`_dual_gram_eigh_from_jacobian`, `engine.py:3380`). Let $K = U \Lambda_M U^\top$ be the eigendecomposition of $K$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_M \ge 0$ and orthonormal eigenvectors $\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_M \in \mathbb{R}^M$. Then:*
>
> 1. *The non-zero spectrum of $G = \frac{1}{M}J^\top J \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ coincides identically with the non-zero spectrum of $K$, and for every $\lambda_r > 10^{-12}$, the vector*
>    $$\mathbf{v}_r = \frac{1}{\sqrt{M \lambda_r}} J^\top \mathbf{u}_r \in \mathbb{R}^d \tag{11}$$
>    *is an exact unit-norm eigenvector of $G$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_r$, computable in $\mathcal{O}(M^2 d + M^3)$ time instead of $\mathcal{O}(d^3)$.*
>
> 2. *Define the **relative-threshold numerical rank** $r_{\sup}(\varepsilon_{\text{null}}) = \bigl|\{m \in \{1, \dots, \min(M, d)\} : \lambda_m(G) > \varepsilon_{\text{null}}\lambda_{\max}(G)\}\bigr|$ with $\varepsilon_{\text{null}} = 10^{-12}$ (`null_rel_tol = 1e-12`). Then the **Regularized Effective Condition Number** restricted to the active numerical support $r_{\sup}(\varepsilon_{\text{null}})$:*
>    $$\kappa_{\text{eff}}(G) = \frac{\lambda_{\max}(G)}{\max\bigl(\lambda_{r_{\sup}(\varepsilon_{\text{null}})}(G),\, 10^{-12}\bigr)} \tag{12}$$
>    *equals $1.0$ on any isotropic subspace of rank $M < d$ (zero false-positive triggers) while accurately detecting extreme multi-scale valleys ($\kappa_{\text{eff}} = 10^8$) even when a single dominant eigenvalue ($\lambda_1 = 1, \lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M = 10^{-8}$) causes the participation ratio $r_{\text{eff}} = (\operatorname{Tr} G)^2/\operatorname{Tr}(G^2) \approx 1.0$ to collapse (`Suite 19h`).*
>
> *Proof.* Multiply $\mathbf{v}_r$ by $G$: $G \mathbf{v}_r = \frac{1}{M} J^\top J \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{M\lambda_r}} J^\top \mathbf{u}_r\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{M\lambda_r}} J^\top \left(\frac{1}{M} J J^\top \mathbf{u}_r\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{M\lambda_r}} J^\top (\lambda_r \mathbf{u}_r) = \lambda_r \mathbf{v}_r$. Orthonormality follows from $\mathbf{v}_r^\top \mathbf{v}_s = \frac{1}{M\sqrt{\lambda_r \lambda_s}} \mathbf{u}_r^\top (J J^\top) \mathbf{u}_s = \frac{\lambda_s}{\sqrt{\lambda_r \lambda_s}} \mathbf{u}_r^\top \mathbf{u}_s = \delta_{rs}$. $\square$

When $\kappa_{\text{eff}}(G) \ge \kappa_{\text{thresh}} = 10^6$, `SpectralFrameLifter` (`engine.py:3720`) queries `SpectralMetaAtlas` using a deterministic 3-branch dispatch rule to apply a coordinate-frame transformation $\Phi \to \Phi'$ with mandatory exact inverse projection $\Phi^{-1}: \mathcal{X}' \to \mathcal{X}$ (and automatically quarantines any operator after 2 consecutive zero-gain probes on a task family):

1. **Minimax Tie-System Dispatch (`KktSlackTransform` with `condense_active_set`):** If the task registers $K_{\text{slack}} \ge 2$ minimax tie constraints ($\min_{1 \le i \le K} f_i(x)$), `SpectralMetaAtlas` dispatches `KktSlackTransform`, lifting $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ to $(x, z) \in \mathbb{R}^{d+K}$ and pruning inactive slack variables whose gap above $z_{\min} = \min_i f_i(x)$ exceeds $f_i(x) - z_{\min} > \sigma_{\text{mult}}\hat{\sigma}_f$, where $\sigma_{\text{mult}} = 0.05$ and $\hat{\sigma}_f = 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}_i(f_i(x) - z_{\min})$. At Heilbronn $n=16$, while the converged KKT optimum has **24 tight equiareal triangles** ($M=24$, Section 7.2), `condense_active_set` with $\sigma_{\text{mult}} = 0.05$ retains **57 near-active triangles** out of $560$ during pre-convergence wake steps (`Trial 1`) so triangles entering or leaving the active boundary are tracked without re-evaluating the 503 far-inactive triangles.
2. **High-Resolution Step-Grid Dispatch (`DyadicHomotopyTransform`):** Else if the parameter vector represents a discretized 1D/2D step function of dimension $P \ge P_{\min} = 160$, `SpectralMetaAtlas` dispatches `DyadicHomotopyTransform` across coarse-to-fine grid levels $P_0 \to P_1 \to \dots \to P$ with block-averaging restriction $\Phi(\mathbf{a})_j = \frac{1}{r}\sum_{l=0}^{r-1} a_{rj+l}$ ($r = P_{k+1}/P_k$) and piecewise-constant/linear prolongation $\Phi^{-1}(\mathbf{a}_c)_{rj+l} = (\mathbf{a}_c)_j$ (or fractional linear interpolation when $P_{k+1}/P_k$ is non-integer), satisfying exact left-invertibility $\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\mathbf{a}_c)) = \mathbf{a}_c$.
3. **General Ill-Conditioned Valley Dispatch (`EigenRotationTransform`):** Otherwise, `SpectralMetaAtlas` dispatches `EigenRotationTransform`, rotating into the stiff active eigen-subspace $\mathbf{y} = V_{\text{active}}^\top(\boldsymbol{\theta} - \boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{anchor}})$, applying diagonal active-mode preconditioning $P = V_{\text{active}}\Lambda_{\text{active}}^{-1/2}$ so the preconditioned Hessian in lifted coordinates has unit condition number $\kappa(P^\top H P) = 1.0$ (`preconditioned_condition_number = 1.0`), and freezing sloppy/null modes ($\lambda_r \le \varepsilon_{\text{null}}\lambda_{\max}$). Across 10 random 8D rotated valleys with $\kappa_{\text{eff}} = 10^8$ and $M=6 < d=8$ (`Trial 2`), this reaches `< 2.5e-7` loss in `15` steps (`1.18 ± 0.04 ms`) vs. `7.1761 ± 5.2062` after `40` steps in unlifted coordinates.

## 5. `SLEEP-DAG-RSI v7`: Autonomous AST Splicing, Active Boundary Synthesis & Hierarchical `SymbolicLibrary`

### 5.1. `AstCodeSplicer`: Multi-Parent Structural Merging & LOO-Validated MDL Input-Routing Synthesis

In earlier evolutionary frameworks, `CROSSOVER(u, v)` merely prompted an LLM with two code strings. In `SLEEP-DAG-RSI v7`, `AstCodeSplicer` (`engine.py:3810`) parses parent Python ASTs via `ast.parse` and performs:

1. **Multi-Parent Structural AST Merge (`structural_ast_merge`):** For $|S| \ge 2$ parents selected by `compute_hyper_crossover`, `AstCodeSplicer` merges all non-colliding helper functions and disjoint intra-function variable assignments ($\operatorname{IoU}(\mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(u), \mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(v)) = 0$) directly into the base syntax tree.
2. **LOO-Validated Nonlinear OOS Input-Routing Predicate Synthesis (`_synthesize_input_routing_predicate`) & Decision-List Cascade for $|S| \ge 3$:** When two parents $(u, v)$ modify the same target expression but specialize in different input regimes (measured by bucket inputs $\mathbf{x}_b \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and parent bucket margins $\phi_b(u), \phi_b(v)$), `AstCodeSplicer` searches a 4-tier hierarchical candidate feature grammar ordered by Minimum Description Length (MDL) complexity:
   $$\mathcal{G}_{\text{route}}(\mathbf{x}) = \Bigl\{\underbrace{x_c,\; |x_c|}_{\text{Tier 1 (1D)}},\; \underbrace{\operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x})}_{\text{Tier 2 (Library)}},\; \underbrace{x_i x_j}_{\text{Tier 3 (Bilinear)}},\; \underbrace{x_i^2 + x_j^2}_{\text{Tier 4 (Radial)}}\Bigr\}, \tag{13}$$
   selecting $(g^\star, \tau^\star)$ to maximize the MDL-penalized excess routing margin objective:
   $$J_{\text{route}}(g, \tau) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{b=1}^M \Bigl(\mathbb{I}[g(\mathbf{x}_b) \ge \tau]\,\phi_b(u) + \mathbb{I}[g(\mathbf{x}_b) < \tau]\,\phi_b(v)\Bigr) - \max\bigl(\bar{\phi}(u), \bar{\phi}(v)\bigr) - \lambda_{\text{MDL}}\,\operatorname{tier}(g), \tag{13a}$$
   where $\operatorname{tier}(g) \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ and $\lambda_{\text{MDL}} = 0.005$. When $|S| \ge 3$ parents modify the same target expression, `AstCodeSplicer` orders the parents $u_{(1)}, \dots, u_{(|S|)}$ by descending peak bucket specialization and recursively synthesizes a sequential `if / elif / else` decision-list cascade $g_m(\mathbf{x}) \ge \tau_m$ at each level $m = 1, \dots, |S|-1$. To prevent overfitting when $M \le 8$ relative to the $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ quadratic candidates in Tiers 3 and 4, `_synthesize_input_routing_predicate` enforces a **Leave-One-Bucket-Out (LOO) Cross-Validation Margin Check**: for each held-out bucket $b \in \{1, \dots, M\}$, the threshold $\tau_{-b}$ fit on the remaining $M-1$ buckets must yield non-negative held-out margin gain on bucket $b$ (`loo_valid = True`, `Suite 19h`), followed by `verify_spliced_epistasis` requiring $s(h_{\text{splice}}) \ge \max_{u \in S} s(u) - \varepsilon_{\text{tol}}$ with $\varepsilon_{\text{tol}} = 10^{-4}$. This cracks both 1D half-space splits (`x[0] >= 0.0`) and bilinear XOR quadrant splits (`(x[0] * x[1]) >= 0.0`) with **`100%`** OOS accuracy while rejecting spurious quadratic fits on random noise (`Suites 18d–18e, 19h`).

### 5.2. `NeuroSymbolicDistiller`, `SymbolicLibrary` & Full-Target Multi-Cycle Re-Consolidation vs. `SINDy` & `Tree-GP SR`

During NREM sleep, `NeuroSymbolicDistiller` (`sleep_dag_rsi.py:865`) extracts explicit Python AST equations from neural slow-expert residuals $\Delta f_m(X) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_{\text{out}}}$ via pure-NumPy Coordinate-Descent Lasso + OLS refitting over a typed basis dictionary $\Psi(X)$ constructed from the top $d_{\text{active}} \le 8$ gradient-selected input coordinates (comprising linear atoms $x_i$, bilinear interactions $x_i x_j$, quadratic terms $x_i^2$, saturating links $\tanh(x_i)$, and regime step gates $\operatorname{sign}(x_i)$, up to $K \le 64$ base atoms). To overcome the depth-2 ceiling of flat sparse regression (*SINDy* STLSQ [Brunton et al., 2016]) and the combinatorial search explosion of unstructured Genetic Programming (`Tree-GP SR` [Cranmer, 2023]), `SLEEP-DAG-RSI v7` introduces two mechanisms:

1. **DreamCoder-Style Hierarchical `SymbolicLibrary` & Outer-Projected Inner Gauss-Newton Search:**
   Whenever a distilled multi-term expression achieves $R^2_{\text{OOS}} \ge 0.85$, it is abstracted into a reusable `LibraryPrimitive` $\operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x})$ storing its natural amplitude scale $s_k = \operatorname{std}(f_{\text{raw}}(X))$ and mean offset $\mu_k$ (so $f_{\text{raw}}(\mathbf{x}) = s_k \operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x}) + \mu_k$). Furthermore, if flat Lasso explains only partial variance ($0.25 \le R^2 < 0.92$) because the true physical law combines a saturating nonlinear core with additive outer linear terms $y = c_1 \tanh(\mathbf{w}_{\text{in}}^\top \boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x})) + \mathbf{w}_{\text{out}}^\top \mathbf{x}_{\text{lin}}$, `_discover_nonlinear_inner_submotif` first restricts the outer linear projection $P_{\text{lin}}$ to pure linear coordinates $x_l$ whose single-feature linear correlation satisfies $|r(x_l, y)| > 0.35$, projects out the orthogonal complement residual $\mathbf{y}_{\text{nl}} = (I - P_{\text{lin}})\mathbf{y}$ (`Suite 19h`), initializes $\mathbf{w}_{\text{in}}^{(0)}$ from the normalized correlation vector of $\mathbf{y}_{\text{nl}}$ with $\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x})$, and executes 5 steps of damped Gauss-Newton ($\lambda_{\text{GN}} = 10^{-3}$) with analytical Jacobian $J_{\tanh} = (1 - \tanh^2(z))\,\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x})$ to jointly refine $(c_1, \mathbf{w}_{\text{in}}, \mathbf{w}_{\text{out}})$ and register the inner argument as $\operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x})$. In subsequent NREM cycles, `SymbolicLibrary` injects nonlinear compositions $\tanh(s_k \operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x}) + \mu_k), \operatorname{sign}(\operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x})), \operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x}) \cdot x_i$, and $\operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \operatorname{sign}(x_i)$ into $\Psi(X)$.

2. **Full-Target Multi-Cycle Re-Consolidation (`reconstruct_uncorrupted_target=True`) & Head-to-Head Comparison vs. Evolutionary `Tree-GP SR`:**
   A subtle mathematical trap occurs when distilling $y = \tanh(z) + 0.6 x_4$ across two sleep cycles: in Cycle 1, flat Lasso fits the best linear approximation $\hat{y}^{(1)} \approx c \cdot z + 0.6 x_4$ ($R^2 \approx 0.86$) and Residual-Keep subtracts it, leaving a **corrupted residual** $\Delta y^{\text{rem}} = \tanh(z) - c \cdot z$ that is no longer a clean $\tanh(z)$ function in Cycle 2! By setting `reconstruct_uncorrupted_target=True`, Cycle $c \ge 2$ reconstructs the uncorrupted target $Y_{\text{full}} = \Delta Y_{\text{rem}} + \sum_{t < c} f_{\text{AST}}^{(t)}(X)$ using `prior_cycle_callable`, re-distills $Y_{\text{full}}$ over the expanded `SymbolicLibrary` dictionary, and **replaces** the prior-cycle AST callables $\{f_{\text{AST}}^{(t)}\}_{t < c}$ with the unified expression $f_{\text{AST}}^{(c)}$ (subtracting $f_{\text{AST}}^{(c)}(X)$ once from $Y_{\text{full}}$ so prior terms are never double-counted). Across 10 random seeds on the depth-3 hierarchical law (`Trial 4` / `Trial 6C`, Table 5), `NeuroSymbolicDistiller + SymbolicLibrary` achieves **$R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 1.0000 \pm 0.0000$** in **`15.8 ms`** per NREM cycle (`34.4 ms` across both cycles), outperforming flat `v6` distillation ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.0400 \pm 0.0717$), *SINDy* STLSQ ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.0251 \pm 0.0793$), and a composition-capable PySR-style evolutionary expression-tree Genetic Programming + OLS/Gauss-Newton baseline (`Tree-GP SR` over $\{+, -, \times, \tanh, \operatorname{sign}\}$ up to depth $\le 4$), which reaches only **$R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.3464 \pm 0.2404$** under a matched **`16.4 ms`** in-loop time budget (`260` evaluations) and **$R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.8486 \pm 0.2397$** even under an extended **`605.2 ms`** (`38x` slower, `9,200` evaluations) evolutionary budget because raw tree search must rediscover the 3-way product $x_3 x_4 \operatorname{sign}(x_7)$ and $\tanh(x_0)\operatorname{sign}(x_7)$ from scratch without `SymbolicLibrary` primitive reuse.

### 5.3. `ActiveBoundarySynthesizer`: Bichromatic Chord Bisection & Committee Variance Ascent

Passive replay buffers cluster inside smooth interior basins and rarely sample regime boundaries where candidate symbolic laws diverge from neural experts. In `SLEEP-DAG-RSI v7`, `ActiveBoundarySynthesizer` (`engine.py:4065`) actively synthesizes high-information boundary probes over the committee $\{f_1, \dots, f_K\}$ (comprising the fast memory model, active slow MoE experts, and distilled AST callables; for continuous regression tasks where outputs do not cross zero, predictions are centered relative to the anchor median $\tilde{f}_k(\mathbf{x}) = f_k(\mathbf{x}) - \operatorname{median}_{\mathbf{x}' \in \mathcal{B}_{\text{anc}}} \bar{f}(\mathbf{x}')$, and gradients $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} f_k(\mathbf{x})$ on discrete AST callables are evaluated via central finite differences with step $h_{\text{FD}} = 10^{-4}$):

1. **Bichromatic Chord Bisection:** Identifies pairs of anchor inputs $(\mathbf{x}_a, \mathbf{x}_b)$ where committee models exhibit opposite centered signs, and performs $T_{\text{bisect}} = 8$ steps of binary chord bisection to isolate exact decision-boundary crossings.
2. **Projected Committee Variance Ascent:** Executes $T_{\text{grad}} = 12$ steps of projected gradient ascent on the ensemble variance $\mathcal{V}(\mathbf{x}) = \operatorname{Var}_{1 \le k \le K}\bigl(f_k(\mathbf{x})\bigr)$ within a local trust ball $\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{anc}}\|_2 \le r_{\text{trust}} = 0.5$. Across `Suite 18b` and `Trial 4` (10 seeds), this amplifies committee disagreement by **`11.00x–20.23x`** over passive replay anchors (`0.1423 -> 2.8785`) and pinpoints exact sign-flipping counterexamples for NREM distillation.

## 6. `SLEEP-DAG-RSI v8`: Six Frontier Upgrades for Extremal Search & Continual Adaptation

In `SLEEP-DAG-RSI v8`, we integrate six mechanisms addressing extremal tail optimization, dead-bucket stagnation, 100%-dead-end NREM batches, long-horizon context bloat, unanimous committee blind spots, and heavy-tailed streaming spikes.

### 6.1. `TTT-Discover` Extremal Max-Tilted Objective (`compute_max_tilted_objective`)

Standard RL and evolutionary portfolios optimize the expected reward $\mathbb{E}[R(y)]$, which favors high-probability mediocre variations over rare, high-variance extremal breakthroughs. Inspired by *TTT-Discover* [Yuksekgonul et al., 2026, `arXiv:2601.16175`], we introduce the scale-normalized **Entropic Max-Tilted Objective** (`engine.py:4295` and `sleep_dag_rsi.py:1348`):
$$J_\tau(R; \theta) = R_{\max} + \frac{\operatorname{stop\_grad}(s_R)}{\tau} \log \left(\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^K \exp\!\left(\tau \frac{R_i - R_{\max}}{\operatorname{stop\_grad}(s_R)}\right)\right) - \beta_{\text{KL}}\,D_{\text{KL}}\bigl(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{\text{ref}}\bigr), \tag{14}$$
$$w_i(\tau) = \frac{\exp\bigl(\tau (R_i - R_{\max})/\operatorname{stop\_grad}(s_R)\bigr)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \exp\bigl(\tau (R_j - R_{\max})/\operatorname{stop\_grad}(s_R)\bigr)}, \qquad s_R = \max\!\Bigl(\operatorname{std}\bigl(\{R_i : R_i \ge \operatorname{median}(R)\}\bigr),\; 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}(R),\; 10^{-6}\Bigr). \tag{14a}$$
Computing $s_R$ from the **upper-tail dispersion above the median** (with a detached `stop_grad(s_R)` flag) ensures that a single catastrophic crash ($R_i = -10^6$) cannot blow up $s_R$ and collapse the effective inverse temperature $\tau / s_R \to 0$ (`Suite 19h`). While the KL divergence penalty $-\beta_{\text{KL}} D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{\text{ref}})$ regularizes parametric policy drift, the trajectory importance weights $w_i(\tau)$ follow the exact Boltzmann tilt in Eq. (14a).

> **Proposition 4 (Monotonic Convergence to the Extremum).** *For any finite sample of rewards $R = (R_1, \dots, R_K) \in \mathbb{R}^K$ and fixed positive scale $s_R > 0$, the tilted expectation $E_\tau(R) = \sum_{i=1}^K w_i(\tau) R_i$ and entropic objective $J_\tau(R)$ (at $\beta_{\text{KL}}=0$) are monotonically non-decreasing in $\tau \ge 0$, with $\frac{\partial E_\tau(R)}{\partial \tau} = \frac{1}{s_R}\operatorname{Var}_{w(\tau)}(R) \ge 0$, $\lim_{\tau \to 0^+} J_\tau(R) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^K R_i$, and $\lim_{\tau \to \infty} J_\tau(R) = \lim_{\tau \to \infty} E_\tau(R) = \max_{1 \le i \le K} R_i$.* $\square$

In `LeanDAGPortfolio.rank_parents_max_tilted`, parent selection weights branches by $w_i(\tau)$, while in `UniversalNeuralSleepAdapter.nrem_consolidate(use_max_tilted_consolidation=True, entropic_tau=4.0)`, NREM gradient steps weight winning trajectories by $w_i(\tau)$, concentrating **`0.9994–1.0000`** of consolidation mass on the true breakthrough branch (`Trial 5` & `Suites 19a, 19g, 19h`).

### 6.2. `BES / LADDER` Backward Sub-Goal Decomposition (`BackwardSubGoalDecomposer`)

When a hard evaluation regime bucket $b^\star \in \{0, \dots, M-1\}$ is a **dead bucket** ($\max_{u \in \mathcal{T}} m_{b^\star}(u) = 0$, i.e., every explored branch has negative margin $\phi_{b^\star}(u) \le 0$), column $b^\star$ of the `pass_mask` matrix is identically zero and `compute_hyper_crossover` cannot discover a synergy bridge. In continuous optimization tasks, $\phi_b(u) = (\tau_b - \ell_b(u))/|\tau_b| \in [-1, 1]$ is the normalized threshold deficit; in discrete code synthesis test suites with binary pass/fail outcomes, $\phi_b(u) \in [-1, 0)$ is defined via the dense execution surrogate margin (fraction of passed sub-assertions minus $1$, negative normalized stacktrace depth, or continuous output distance prior to binary thresholding). Combining *Backward Exploratory Search (BES)* [arXiv:2509.21077] and *LADDER* [Simonds & Yoshiyama, 2025, `arXiv:2503.00735`], `BackwardSubGoalDecomposer` (`engine.py:2218`) identifies the closest near-miss anchor node $u_{\text{anc}}^\star = \arg\max_u \phi_{b^\star}(u) < 0$ and synthesizes a **3-rung stepping-stone ladder** ($k \in \{1, 2, 3\}$) with progressively tightened margin thresholds $\tau_{b^\star}^{(k)} = \alpha_k \phi_{b^\star}(u_{\text{anc}}^\star)$ ($\alpha_k \in \{0.5, 0.25, 0.0\}$), monotonically increasing problem scales $N^{(k)} = \max\bigl(1, \lfloor N / 2^{4-k}\rfloor\bigr)$ ($N^{(1)} < N^{(2)} < N^{(3)} \le N$), and relaxed tolerances $\epsilon^{(k)} = 10^{4-k}\epsilon$. Any candidate clearing the active rung $\phi_{b^\star}(v) \ge \tau_{b^\star}^{(k)}$ is promoted by `select_stepping_stone_seed` (`engine.py:2390`) as the root seed `promoted_root_seed_id` for the next round (`Suite 19b`).

### 6.3. `SDPO` Feedback-Conditioned Self-Teacher on 100%-Dead-End Batches (`FeedbackConditionedSelfTeacher`)

In `SLEEP-DAG-RSI v5`, when an exploratory Wake batch on a hard domain yields $100\%$ dead-ends ($\sum_i m_{\text{win}}(u_i) = 0$), `DAG-KS` sets `loss_mask = 0` everywhere, wasting the entire NREM cycle. Inspired by *Self-Distillation with Policy Optimization (SDPO)* [Hübotter et al., 2026, `arXiv:2601.20802`], `FeedbackConditionedSelfTeacher` (`sleep_dag_rsi.py:1128`) turns rich verifier diagnostics $\Delta_i = \Delta_{\text{verifier}}(\mathbf{x}_i, f_i)$ (directional residuals, constraint violations, or stacktrace corrections) on failed attempts $\mathbf{y}_i^{\text{attempt}}$ into corrected self-teacher targets:
$$\hat{\mathbf{y}}_i^{\text{teacher}} = \mathbf{y}_i^{\text{attempt}} + \gamma_i \cdot \Delta_i, \qquad \omega_i^{\text{SDPO}} = \omega_{\max} \cdot w_{\text{Tukey}}(d_i) \cdot \frac{r_i}{r_i + m_r}, \tag{15}$$
where $r_i = \|\Delta_i\|_2$ and $m_r = \operatorname{median}_i(r_i)$. To protect NREM consolidation against identical-assertion ties, non-convex kinks, and adversarial verifier corruption up to and beyond the $50\%$ breakdown point, we enforce four safeguards:

1. **Zero-Residual Abstention Gate (`abstained_zero_win = True`):** Attempts with $r_i \le 10^{-8}$ carry zero directional information and receive $\omega_i^{\text{SDPO}} = 0$. Whenever `direct_win_count == 0` and `sdpo_reclaimed_count == 0` (either because all verifier residuals are zero or because corruption triggers fallback), `nrem_consolidate` abstains completely (`sample_weights = 0`, `abstained_zero_win = True`) so slow-expert weights remain strictly untouched rather than fitting failed dead-end trajectories (`Suites 19g, 19h`).
2. **Iterative Tukey IRLS Center $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{IRLS}}$, MAD Floor Guard & Signed Cosine Gate:** Given $m_r = \operatorname{median}_i(r_i)$ and $\operatorname{MAD}_r = \operatorname{median}_i(|r_i - m_r|)$, when $\ge 50\%$ of dead-end attempts fail on the exact same assertion with identical residual magnitude, $\operatorname{MAD}_r = 0$ identically. To prevent $0/0$ rejection of valid inliers, we enforce the scale floor $\sigma_{\text{MAD}} = \max(1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}_r,\; 0.15\,m_r,\; 10^{-8})$ (`Suite 19h`) and refine a robust consensus shift vector $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{IRLS}}$ over $T_{\text{IRLS}} = 3$ Tukey biweight iterations initialized at the coordinate-wise median $\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(0)} = \operatorname{median}_i(\Delta_i)$. With $d_i = \|\Delta_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{IRLS}}\|_2$, $m_d = \operatorname{median}_i(d_i)$, and $\sigma_d = \max(1.4826\,\operatorname{median}_i|d_i - m_d|,\; 0.15\,m_r,\; 10^{-8})$, any sample with $z_i = |d_i - m_d|/\sigma_d > c_{\text{MAD}}$ ($c_{\text{MAD}} = 3.0$) or signed cosine similarity $\cos(\Delta_i, \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{IRLS}}) < \tau_{\cos} = 0.25$ is rejected ($w_{\text{Tukey}}(d_i) = 0$), while inliers receive $w_{\text{Tukey}}(d_i) = \bigl(1 - (z_i / c_{\text{MAD}})^2\bigr)^2$.
3. **Bilateral Crease Criterion & Piecewise-Linear Basis for Non-Convex Kinks:** When failed attempts straddle a non-smooth V-shaped cliff (e.g., $y^\star(\mathbf{x}) = -|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}|$, where opposite-side shifts point in antipodal directions $\pm\mathbf{g}$ and would be falsely rejected by the one-sided cosine gate $\cos(\Delta_i, \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\text{IRLS}}) \ge 0.25$), `reclaim_vector_dead_ends` (`sleep_dag_rsi.py:1210`) tests a three-part **Bilateral Crease Criterion**: (i) projecting centered shifts $\tilde{\Delta}_i = \Delta_i - \bar{\Delta}$ onto their principal SVD axis $\mathbf{u}_1(\Delta)$ partitions samples into balanced antipodal halves $I_+ = \{i : \langle \tilde{\Delta}_i, \mathbf{u}_1\rangle \ge 0\}$ and $I_- = \{i : \langle \tilde{\Delta}_i, \mathbf{u}_1\rangle < 0\}$ with $\min(|I_+|, |I_-|) \ge 0.20 N$ and $\cos(\bar{\Delta}_+, \bar{\Delta}_-) < -0.50$; (ii) fitting a centered piecewise-linear crease model $\hat{f}_{\text{crease}}(\mathbf{x}_i) = W_{\text{crease}}\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\text{crease}}(\mathbf{x}_i)$ over $\boldsymbol{\psi}_{\text{crease}}(\mathbf{x}_i) = [\mathbf{x}_i,\; |\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{c}|,\; 1]^\top$ ($\mathbf{c} \in \{\mathbf{0}, \operatorname{median}_i(\mathbf{x}_i)\}$) achieves spatial separability $R^2_{\text{crease}} \ge 0.85$ on the candidate targets $\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_i = \mathbf{y}_i^{\text{attempt}} + \gamma_i \Delta_i$ (preventing spatially unseparated 50/50 sign-flip corruption at the same $\mathbf{x}$ from masquerading as a valid crease); and (iii) the median crease residual $m_b = \operatorname{median}_i\|\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_i - \hat{f}_{\text{crease}}(\mathbf{x}_i)\|_2$ satisfies $m_b < \min(0.10\,m_r, 0.25\,m_e)$, where $m_e$ is the linear residual median:
   $$\min\bigl(|I_+|, |I_-|\bigr) \ge 0.20 N, \qquad \cos\bigl(\bar{\Delta}_+, \bar{\Delta}_-\bigr) < -0.50, \qquad R^2_{\text{crease}} = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N \|\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_i - \hat{f}_{\text{crease}}(\mathbf{x}_i)\|_2^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N \|\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_i - \bar{\mathbf{y}}\|_2^2} \ge 0.85. \tag{15a}$$
   Only when all three conditions hold does the teacher switch to axial alignment $|\cos(\Delta_i, \mathbf{u}_1)| \ge \tau_{\cos}$ and evaluate inlier weights from the crease residual (`r2_crease = 0.9985 >= 0.85`, `opposing_cluster_cosine < -0.50`, `Suite 19h`).
4. **Validation-Anchor Safety Fallback (`clean_anchor_xy`):** Because median/MAD estimators have a theoretical breakdown point of $50\%$, whenever a clean validation anchor is available and the proposed SDPO update would increase anchor loss (which occurs when corruption $\ge 50\%$), the teacher triggers `fallback_triggered = True`, abstains from slow-expert updates (`abstained_zero_win = True`), and preserves the pre-update weights (`Trial 6`, Table 6).

On a $100\%$-dead-end batch (`Trial 5`), `FeedbackConditionedSelfTeacher` reclaims `32/32` failed trajectories and slashes slow-expert MSE from **`15.8726` to `0.0985` (`161.15x` reduction)**; in our 10-seed corruption sweep (`Trial 6`), `SDPO` achieves **`MSE = 0.0000 ± 0.0000`** across $0\%, 10\%$, and $25\%$ corruption, degrades gracefully at $40\%$, and triggers the safety fallback on **`80%–100%`** of seeds at $50\%–60\%$ corruption.

### 6.4. `MutableSkillState` (`SKILL.state`): Fixed-Capacity Cross-Cycle Memory

Appending raw round traces to LLM prompts causes $\mathcal{O}(T)$ context bloat and attention dilution over long horizons. `MutableSkillState` (`engine.py:2456`) maintains a schema-validated, bounded JSON state updated atomically at the end of each cycle via `commit_cycle(tree)` with explicit capacity caps and deterministic eviction policies:

* `best_invariants` ($K_{\text{inv}} = 8$ in compact mode, $16$ in extended profile): deduplicated by `(node_id, tunable_params)` signature and sorted by `(score, updated_round)` descending, evicting the lowest-scoring / oldest invariant once capacity is reached;
* `dead_end_fingerprints` ($K_{\text{dead}} = 16$): deduplicated by `(fail_class, pass_mask_signature, error_summary)` with cumulative `occurrences` counters and sorted by `(occurrences, last_round)` descending, evicting the lowest-frequency / oldest failure signature;
* `unsolved_buckets` ($K_{\text{buck}} = 8$ in compact profile, up to $32$ max): tracks active stepping-stone targets from `decompose_dead_buckets([tree])`, **automatically evicts** any bucket $b$ as soon as a node achieves $\phi_b(u) > 0$, and sorts remaining dead buckets by `current_best_margin` ascending (evicting the smallest-deficit bucket when full);
* `active_library_primitives` ($K_{\text{prim}} = 8$ in compact profile, $12$ in extended profile): reusable AST functions from `SymbolicLibrary` sorted by `compression_gain` / OOS $R^2$ descending, evicting the lowest-gain primitive.

All floats pass through an RFC-8259 sanitizer (`_sanitize_json_float`), guaranteeing strict JSON serializability (`allow_nan=False`) even when nodes report `-inf` or `NaN` scores, while `validate_schema()` verifies that zero raw trace keys (`raw_trace`, `stdout_log`, `stderr_log`, `line_hotspots`) leak into the state. Over 50 discovery rounds (`Trial 5`), `SKILL.state` requires **`1,348` bytes** versus `74,820` bytes for an append-only log (**`-98.20%` compression**).

### 6.5. `VeriHarness` Dual-Head Verification: `Disagreement Resolver` + Smooth Signed-Margin `Consensus Challenger`

Standard active learning (`ActiveBoundarySynthesizer` in `v7`) searches for points where committee models $f_1, \dots, f_K$ disagree ($\max_{\mathbf{x}} \operatorname{Var}_k(f_k(\mathbf{x}))$). However, when all committee members share the same architectural inductive bias, they suffer from **unanimous blind spots**: $\operatorname{Var}_k(f_k(\mathbf{x})) \approx 0$ even right next to a decision cliff where the entire ensemble is wrong!

Inspired by *VeriHarness* [arXiv:2603.17906], `synthesize_dual_head_verification` (`engine.py:6424`) pairs **Head A (`Disagreement Resolver`)** with **Head B (`Consensus Challenger`)**. Rather than optimizing a ratio $|\bar{f}(\mathbf{x})|/\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_k}$ (whose denominator gradient diverges as $\operatorname{Var}_k \to 0$ and stalls at the non-differentiable $|\bar{f}| = 0$ crease), Head B performs $T_{\text{ascent}} = 8$ steps of projected central finite-difference gradient descent (step size $\eta_{\text{step}} = 0.20\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{anc}}$, finite-difference stencil $h_{\text{FD}} = \max(10^{-5}, 10^{-3}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{anc}})$, $\lambda_{\text{var}} = 2.0$, $\epsilon = 10^{-12}$) within the local coordinate-scaled trust hyperbox $\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{anc}}\|_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\text{anc}}^{-1}, \infty} \le r_{\text{trust}} = 1.5$ to **minimize the smooth signed consensus margin**:
$$\mathcal{M}_{\text{cons}}(\mathbf{x}) = s_{\text{anc}} \cdot \bar{f}(\mathbf{x}) + \lambda_{\text{var}}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_k\bigl(f_k(\mathbf{x})\bigr) + \epsilon}, \qquad s_{\text{anc}} = \operatorname{sign}\bigl(\bar{f}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{anc}})\bigr) \in \{-1, +1\}, \quad \bar{f}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K f_k(\mathbf{x}). \tag{16}$$
Because $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{M}_{\text{cons}}(\mathbf{x}) = s_{\text{anc}}\nabla\bar{f}(\mathbf{x}) + \frac{\lambda_{\text{var}}}{2\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_k + \epsilon}}\nabla\operatorname{Var}_k(\mathbf{x})$ is smooth and non-vanishing even when $\operatorname{Var}_k(f_k(\mathbf{x})) = 0$ identically, descending $-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{M}_{\text{cons}}$ pushes $\mathbf{x}$ directly across the zero-level set into the negative signed-margin region ($s_{\text{anc}}\bar{f}(\mathbf{x}) < 0$) where all committee models flip sign in unison. When an executable ground-truth verifier $f^\star(\mathbf{x})$ (`ground_truth_oracle_fn`) is supplied, `synthesize_dual_head_verification` additionally certifies `oracle_verified_blind_spots` where all $K$ committee members agree on the wrong sign relative to $f^\star(\mathbf{x})$ (`Suites 19e, 19h`).

### 6.6. `FALCON-2` Prefix-Aligned NLMS Fast-Weight Adaptation vs. `Gated DeltaNet`

In `DeltaRuleFastMemory` (`v5`), the unnormalized outer-product update $\Delta M_t = \eta_t (\mathbf{v}_t - M_{t-1}\mathbf{k}_t)\mathbf{k}_t^\top$ (where $\mathbf{v}_t = \mathbf{y}_t^{\text{raw}} - f_{\text{base}}(\mathbf{x}_t)$ denotes the base-subtracted target residual `cand.y_stream - cand.base_out`) scales quadratically with input norm ($\propto \|\mathbf{x}_t\|_2^2$). A single $10^3\times$ sensor spike causes immediate numerical overflow (`loss = 8.18e14`). Furthermore, naive prefix mixing $\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_t = (1-\alpha)\mathbf{k}_t + \alpha \mathbf{k}_{t-1}$ (used in standard *Gated DeltaNet* [Yang et al., 2025]) suffers from catastrophic norm cancellation when an opposite-sign spike arrives ($\mathbf{x}_t \approx -\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{t-1} \implies \|\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_t\|_2 \to 0$).

Inspired by *FALCON-2* [Meng, Behrouz, Mirrokni, & Razaviyayn, 2026, `arXiv:2603.21456`], we implement **Prefix-Aligned Normalized Least-Mean-Squares (NLMS)** with a **running prefix average of unit-normalized keys** $\bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t-1}$, cosine alignment gate $\alpha_{\text{prefix}}(t)$ (default token-memory constants $\eta_t = 0.05, \lambda_{\text{NLMS}} = 1.0, \alpha_{\min} = 0.10, c_{\text{innov}} = 10.0$ in `DeltaRuleFastMemory.forward_falcon2`, `sleep_dag_rsi.py:406`; and batch fast-adapter constants $\eta_t = 0.15, \alpha_{\min} = 0.05$ in `wake_adapt_step_falcon2`, `sleep_dag_rsi.py:2202`), max-norm denominator, and **Huber innovation clipping**:
$$\Delta M_t = \frac{\eta_t \, \alpha_{\text{prefix}}(t)}{\max\bigl(\|\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_t\|_2^2,\, \|\mathbf{k}_t\|_2^2\bigr) + \lambda_{\text{NLMS}}} \,\operatorname{clip}_{\text{Huber}}\bigl(\mathbf{v}_t - M_{t-1}\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_t,\; c_{\text{innov}}\bigr)\,\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_t^\top, \qquad \alpha_{\text{prefix}}(t) = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{1 + \cos(\hat{\mathbf{k}}_t, \bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t-1})}{2},\; \alpha_{\min},\; 1.0\right), \tag{17}$$
$$\hat{\mathbf{k}}_t = \frac{\mathbf{k}_t}{\|\mathbf{k}_t\|_2 + 10^{-12}}, \qquad \bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t} = \frac{t - 1}{t}\,\bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t-1} + \frac{1}{t}\,\hat{\mathbf{k}}_t, \qquad \tilde{\mathbf{k}}_t = \bigl(1 - \alpha_{\text{prefix}}(t)\bigr)\mathbf{k}_t + \alpha_{\text{prefix}}(t)\,\|\mathbf{k}_t\|_2\,\frac{\bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t-1}}{\|\bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t-1}\|_2 + 10^{-12}}. \tag{17a}$$
Normalizing each raw key $\hat{\mathbf{k}}_t = \mathbf{k}_t / (\|\mathbf{k}_t\|_2 + 10^{-12})$ before updating the running prefix mean $\bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t}$ prevents a single $10^3\times$ covariate spike from hijacking the context direction, while Huber innovation clipping ($c_{\text{innov}} = 10.0$) bounds fast-weight updates under extreme target-value outliers ($y_t = 10^3$). Across 10 random seeds (`Trial 6`, Table 6), `FALCON-2` achieves **`13.5x` lower post-spike loss** than *Gated DeltaNet* under opposite-sign `[1000v, -333.3v]` cancellation spikes (`41,816.21` vs. `564,478.28`) and **`101.9x` lower fast-weight blowup** under $10^3\times$ target outliers ($\|B_{\text{fast}}\|_F = 3.25$ vs. `331.04`).

    # --- PHASE 3 & 4: REM DREAMING, SKILL.STATE COMMIT & 2x2 JOINT ACCEPTANCE GATE ---

    return accept_joint_2x2(theta_0, theta_plus, psi_0, psi_plus, min_novelty_floor=0.05)

### 7.2. Theorem 1: Resolution of the 1D Equiareal Tie-System Trap at Heilbronn $n = 13$ and $n = 16$

In Heilbronn's triangle problem, $n$ points $\mathbf{p}_0, \dots, \mathbf{p}_{n-1}$ are placed in a unit-area triangle $T$ to maximize $\Delta_n = \min_{0 \le i < j < k < n} \operatorname{Area}(\mathbf{p}_i, \mathbf{p}_j, \mathbf{p}_k)$. Because global optimality over all $\binom{n}{3}$ combinatorial triangle topologies remains open for $n \ge 7$, numerical discovery seeks **certified local KKT stationary optima** $\Delta_n^\star$ on the best discovered active combinatorial topology, paired with exact $10^{100}$-denominator rational vertex roundings $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i \in \mathbb{Q}^2 \cap T$ that certify rigorous constructive lower bounds $\underline{\Delta}_n = \min_{i<j<k}\operatorname{Area}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i, \hat{\mathbf{p}}_j, \hat{\mathbf{p}}_k) \le \Delta_n$ ($|\Delta_n^\star - \underline{\Delta}_n| < 10^{-99}$). Prior records at $n=13$ (`0.026556528919738264474389245471`, Tej Stead, July 2026, $M=20$ active triangles) and $n=16$ (`0.0183555463554034501359529610459`, Fable 5.1 & Shengtong Zhang, Sept 2026, $M=24$ active triangles) were refined to 30–80 digits by solving the area-equality tie system $A_1(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = A_2(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \dots = A_M(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ via damped Gauss-Newton, assuming the tie system defines an isolated 0-dimensional point.

> **Theorem 1 (Corank-1 Equiareal Manifold $\Gamma(s)$ and Bordered KKT-Newton Resolution at $n=13$ and $n=16$).** *Let $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{free}}}$ denote the free vertex coordinates of the active frame after fixing the 6 boundary constraints (2 vertices on each triangle side, all of whose outward normal KKT multipliers are strictly dual-feasible $\mu_{\text{boundary}, r}^\star > 0$ so no boundary vertex can improve the minimum area by moving inward):*
>
> 1. **At $n = 16$ ($15 + 1$ Floater Split):** *Exactly 15 vertices form the active frame ($d_{\text{free}} = 2\times 15 - 6 = 24$) carrying all $M = 24$ minimal triangles at the converged optimum, while the 16th vertex (`p_13 = (0.440423714855, 0.491088435790)`, corresponding to `vertex 1` in `math.tejstead.com/heilbronn/triangle/16/`) is a strict interior floater participating in **zero** active triangles with Voronoi cell clearance $\mathbf{0.0185320014278944} > \Delta_{16}^\star + 1.7645 \times 10^{-4}$.*
> 2. **Corank-1 Equiareal Curve $\Gamma(s)$:** *In both $n=13$ ($d_{\text{free}} = 20, M = 20$) and $n=16$ ($d_{\text{free}} = 24, M = 24$), the $M \times d_{\text{free}}$ active area Jacobian $J = \frac{\partial(A_1, \dots, A_M)}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}}$ (with $m$-th row $J_m = \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} A_m(\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d_{\text{free}}}$) at the KKT stationary point $\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star$ has rank $d_{\text{free}} - 1$ ($\operatorname{rank}(J_{13}) = 19 < 20$, $\|J_{13}\mathbf{v}_0\|_2 = 1.03\times 10^{-551}$; and $\operatorname{rank}(J_{16,\text{frame}}) = 23 < 24$, $\|J_{16,\text{frame}}\mathbf{v}_0\|_2 = 9.08\times 10^{-552}$). Moreover, let $L = [-\mathbf{1}_{M-1},\; I_{M-1}] \in \mathbb{R}^{(M-1)\times M}$ denote the tie-difference operator mapping $\mathbf{A}(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ to $A_m(\boldsymbol{\theta}) - A_1(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ ($m=2,\dots,M$). Because $\ker(J^\top) = \operatorname{span}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star)$ with $\mathbf{1}_M^\top \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star = 1 \ne 0$ and $\ker(L^\top) = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{1}_M)$, any $\mathbf{u} \in \ker((LJ)^\top)$ satisfies $J^\top(L^\top \mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{0} \implies L^\top \mathbf{u} = \alpha \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star$; taking the inner product with $\mathbf{1}_M$ yields $0 = (\mathbf{1}_M^\top L^\top)\mathbf{u} = \alpha(\mathbf{1}_M^\top \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star) = \alpha$, so $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0}$ and $\operatorname{rank}(LJ) = M - 1$ has full row rank. By the Implicit Function Theorem, the $M-1$ tie-system equations $L\mathbf{A}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathbf{0}$ define a **smooth 1-dimensional equiareal curve** $\Gamma(s) = \boldsymbol{\theta}^\star + s\mathbf{v}_0 + s^2\mathbf{w} + \mathcal{O}(s^3)$ along the unit right nullvector $\mathbf{v}_0 \in \ker J$ on which all $M$ triangles have strictly equal area $\Delta(s)$ to arbitrary precision.*
> 3. **Parameterization-Independent KKT Curvature $c > 0$ and Nonsingular Bordered KKT-Newton System:** *Because $\ker(J^\top)$ is 1-dimensional and spanned by strictly positive KKT multipliers $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star > 0$ ($\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^\star = 1$, $J^\top \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star = \mathbf{0}$), multiplying the second-order Taylor expansion $A_m(\boldsymbol{\theta}(s)) = \Delta^\star + s J_m \mathbf{v}_0 + s^2 \left(J_m \mathbf{w} + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{v}_0^\top \nabla^2 A_m \mathbf{v}_0\right) + \mathcal{O}(s^3)$ by $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\star\top}$ annihilates the normal correction $\mathbf{w}$ identically ($\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\star\top} J \mathbf{w} = 0$), yielding:*
>    $$\Delta(s) = \Delta^\star - c \cdot s^2 + \mathcal{O}(s^3), \qquad c = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^\star \,\mathbf{v}_0^\top \nabla^2 A_m(\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star)\mathbf{v}_0 > 0, \tag{18}$$
>    *with certified values $c_{13} = \mathbf{0.036078780237794575\dots} > 0$ and $c_{16} = \mathbf{0.011234563953840429\dots} > 0$. Running damped Gauss-Newton on the tie system from a `float64` iterate at arc-length offset $s_0 \sim 10^{-6}$ projects orthogonally onto $\Gamma(s)$ at $s \approx s_0 \ne 0$ and terminates immediately with zero tie residual at a value $c s_0^2$ below the apex. In contrast, the $(d_{\text{free}} + M + 1) \times (d_{\text{free}} + M + 1)$ **bordered KKT-Newton system**:*
>    $$\begin{pmatrix} H_{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) & J(\boldsymbol{\theta})^\top & \mathbf{0} \\ J(\boldsymbol{\theta}) & \mathbf{0} & -\mathbf{1}_M \\ \mathbf{0}^\top & \mathbf{1}_M^\top & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \delta\boldsymbol{\theta} \\ \delta\boldsymbol{\lambda} \\ \delta\Delta \end{pmatrix} = -\begin{pmatrix} J(\boldsymbol{\theta})^\top \boldsymbol{\lambda} \\ \mathbf{A}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) - \Delta\mathbf{1}_M \\ \mathbf{1}_M^\top \boldsymbol{\lambda} - 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad H_{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) = \sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m \nabla^2 A_m(\boldsymbol{\theta}), \tag{18a}$$
>    *is **strictly non-singular** at $s = 0$ even though $\operatorname{rank}(J) = d_{\text{free}} - 1$: if $(\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}, \delta\boldsymbol{\lambda}, \delta\Delta)$ lies in the nullspace of the left-hand matrix, multiplying the second block row $J\delta\boldsymbol{\theta} - \delta\Delta\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$ on the left by $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\star\top}$ gives $-\delta\Delta(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\star\top}\mathbf{1}_M) = -\delta\Delta = 0$, so $\delta\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \ker J = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{v}_0)$, say $\delta\boldsymbol{\theta} = \alpha\mathbf{v}_0$. Multiplying the first block row $H_{\mathcal{L}}\delta\boldsymbol{\theta} + J^\top\delta\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \mathbf{0}$ on the left by $\mathbf{v}_0^\top$ gives $\alpha(\mathbf{v}_0^\top H_{\mathcal{L}}\mathbf{v}_0) = -2c\alpha = 0 \implies \alpha = 0$ ($\delta\boldsymbol{\theta} = \mathbf{0}$), whence $J^\top\delta\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \mathbf{0} \implies \delta\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \beta\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star$, and the third row $\mathbf{1}_M^\top\delta\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \beta = 0$ forces $\delta\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \mathbf{0}$. Solving Eq. (18a) in 560-digit arithmetic converges quadratically to the exact stationary apex $s = 0$ ($\|J^\top \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star\|_2 < 10^{-548}$), yielding:*
>    $$\Delta_{13}^\star = \mathbf{0.02655652891977558013836441122594677335105195594906545441276197643\dots} \quad (\mathbf{+3.731566 \times 10^{-14}} \text{ at } s_0 = 1.0170 \times 10^{-6}), \tag{18b}$$
>    $$\Delta_{16}^\star = \mathbf{0.01835554635546114041232536365340313429459832905287243778082350191\dots} \quad (\mathbf{+5.769028 \times 10^{-14}} \text{ at } s_0 = 2.2661 \times 10^{-6}). \quad \square \tag{18c}$$

### 7.4. Extremal Autocorrelation Inequalities ($C_1, C_2, \Phi_3$)

For step functions $f(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{P-1} a_i \mathbb{I}_{I_i}(x)$ on $[-1/4, 1/4]$ with discrete autoconvolution $g_k = (a * a)_k = \sum_{i+j=k} a_i a_j$ ($k \in \{0, \dots, 2P-2\}$) and symmetric autocorrelation $c_0 = \sum_{i=0}^{P-1} a_i^2$, $c_k = 2\sum_{i-j=k} a_i a_j$ ($k \ne 0$, where the factor of $2$ accounts for both $(i, j)$ and $(j, i)$ pairs):

1. **First Autocorrelation Inequality ($C_1$, Cartridge 3):** Minimizing $C_1(a) = \frac{2P \max_k g_k}{(\sum_i a_i)^2}$ ($a_i \ge 0$). Using exact Lagrangian sphere curvature $2\max(g_k - \mu, 0)$ + `Micro-Wakeup` + 4-stage dyadic prolongation ($P=160 \to 2560$) and `RRSI Pruner` (keeping `3/5` active scar-healing knobs), we certify the single-grid step function `autocorr_c1_champion.npy` at $P=2560$:
   $$C_1 \le \mathbf{1.503822792008} \quad \left(\text{exact } 10^{16}\text{-rational } \frac{341010539074195724916434565120000}{226762409866468403805013573068561}\right), \tag{20}$$
   certified across all $2P-1 = 5,119$ shifts via Kronecker polynomial squaring in `fractions.Fraction` at $10^{16}$ and $10^{50}$. Within the $P \le 2,560$ single-step-function regime, this improves upon Matolcsi & Vinuesa [2010] (`1.5098`) by `-0.005977` and Google DeepMind *AlphaEvolve v1* [Novikov et al., 2025] (`1.5053`) by `-0.001477` (whereas recent variable-interval / ultra-high-resolution $P \ge 30,000$ continuous and step constructions reach $1.50314$ in *ThetaEvolve* [Wang et al., 2025] and *AlphaEvolve v2* [Georgiev et al., 2025], $1.502870$ in Khrulkov et al. [2026], and $1.50286$ in *TTT-Discover* [Yuksekgonul et al., 2026]).

2. **Second Autocorrelation Inequality ($C_2$, Cartridge 7):** Maximizing over nonnegative step weights $a_i \ge 0$ (`verify_extremal_cartridges_7_8.py:130`):
   $$C_2(a) = \frac{\|f*f\|_2^2}{\|f*f\|_1 \|f*f\|_\infty} = \frac{2\sum_{k=0}^{2P-2} g_k^2 + 2\sum_{k=0}^{2P-3} g_k g_{k+1}}{3\bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{2P-2} g_k\bigr)\max_{0 \le k \le 2P-2} g_k} \ge \mathbf{0.942158772814} \qquad (P = 20,256,\; a_i \ge 0). \tag{21}$$
   Near the optimum at $P = 20,256$ (`autocorr_c2_p20256_champion.npy`), **`50.93%` (`20,632 / 40,511`)** of convolution nodes lie on a flat roof $g_k \ge 0.95 g_{\max}$, where single-index `argmax` subgradients play whack-a-mole across competing peaks. Our **Boltzmann Roof-Bundle Subgradient** replaces $\max_k g_k$ with $\widehat{g}_{\max}(\tau) = \sum_k \operatorname{stop\_grad}(w_k(\tau)) g_k$, $w_k(\tau) = \operatorname{softmax}((g_k - g_{\max})/\tau)$ annealed over $\tau \in [8\times 10^{-6}, 2\times 10^{-5}] g_{\max}$, progressing from $C_2 \ge 0.93193310$ ($P = 633$) to $C_2 \ge 0.942111374293$ ($P = 10,128$) and $C_2 \ge \mathbf{0.942158772814}$ ($P = 20,256$), surpassing Matolcsi & Vinuesa (`0.88922`), AlphaEvolve v1 (`0.90530`), and Jaech & Joseph [2025] (`0.94136` at $P=2399$) by **`+0.00079877`** within the $P \le 20,256$ step-function regime (complementary to ultra-fine $P \ge 50,000$ multi-GPU searches reaching $0.9595$ in *TTT-Discover* [Yuksekgonul et al., 2026] and $0.9610$ in *AlphaEvolve v2* [Georgiev et al., 2025]).

3. **Third Autocorrelation Inequality for Signed Functions ($\Phi_3$, Problem 4 of *Dream-RSI*):** Minimizing $\Phi_3(a) = \max_k |g_k|$ where $g_k = \frac{2}{P}(a*a)_k$ subject to $\frac{1}{P}\sum_i a_i = 1$ ($a_i \in \mathbb{R}$). For the smooth bidirectional LogSumExp potential $L_\beta(a) = \frac{1}{\beta}\log \sum_k (\exp(\beta g_k) + \exp(-\beta g_k))$ with partition function $Z(\beta) = \sum_k (\exp(\beta g_k) + \exp(-\beta g_k))$, let $u_k(\beta) = \frac{\exp(\beta g_k) + \exp(-\beta g_k)}{Z(\beta)} \ge 0$ ($\sum_k u_k = 1$) denote the total shift probability and $w_k(\beta) = \frac{\exp(\beta g_k) - \exp(-\beta g_k)}{Z(\beta)} \in [-u_k, u_k]$ denote the signed shift weight. Dropping the indefinite Hankel second-derivative term $\sum_k w_k \nabla^2 g_k$ yields an exact analytical **Positive Semi-Definite (PSD) Gauss-Newton preconditioner**:
   $$H_{\text{GN}}(a) = \beta J(a)^\top \bigl(\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{u}(\beta)) - \mathbf{w}(\beta)\mathbf{w}(\beta)^\top\bigr) J(a) \succeq 0, \qquad J_{k,i}(a) = \frac{\partial g_k}{\partial a_i} = \frac{4}{P} a_{k-i}, \tag{22}$$
   where positive semi-definiteness follows from $|w_k| \le u_k$ and Cauchy–Schwarz ($\mathbf{z}^\top\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{u})\mathbf{z} \ge \sum_k |w_k| z_k^2 \ge (\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{z})^2$). Coupling $H_{\text{GN}}(a)$ with full-Hessian Newton at $\beta = 5\times 10^6$ ($P = 3,200$, `phi3_best_candidate.npy`) establishes $\Phi_3 \le \mathbf{1.457058511867}$, outperforming OpenEvolve (`1.4600`), EvoX (`1.4589`), Cilleruelo–Ruzsa–Vinuesa (`1.4581`), and ShinkaEvolve (`1.4578`).

## 9. Limitations, Failure Modes & Broader Impact

In accordance with rigorous peer-review standards, we explicitly document the limitations and boundary conditions of `SLEEP-DAG-RSI v8`:

1. **Cold-Start Discovery Compute (Stage A) vs. Replay Verification Time (Stage B):**
   While our verification and replay scripts (`auto_dream_rsi.py`, `verify_extremal_cartridges_7_8.py`, `heilbronn_pr_package/verify_pr_package.py`) execute in `0.4s–3.9s`, they operate over persisted intermediate checkpoints produced during Stage A cold-start search. Discovering the optimal topological chambers for Heilbronn $n=13\dots 17$ from random `std::mt19937_64` seeds or polishing $P=20,256$ step convolutions via `Boltzmann Roof-Bundle Subgradient` requires `12–95 minutes` of multi-threaded C++ and GPU compute (Table 2a).

2. **Expressivity Boundaries of `NeuroSymbolicDistiller` & `SymbolicLibrary`:**
   Our hierarchical distiller uses a strictly bounded grammar ($d_{\text{active}} \le 8$ gradient-selected inputs, $K \le 64$ basis atoms per expert) and relies on smooth 5-step Gauss-Newton refinement (`_discover_nonlinear_inner_submotif`) to crack inner arguments of saturating links $\tanh(\cdot)$. Laws involving deeply nested non-differentiable combinatorics (e.g., bitwise hashes, modulo arithmetic, or recursive data structures) cannot be extracted via sparse coordinate-descent Lasso and remain inside the residual neural weights $\Delta f_m^{\text{rem}}$ or require discrete LLM code synthesis.

3. **Diagnostic Richness & Breakdown Point in `SDPO` (`FeedbackConditionedSelfTeacher`):**
   Reclaiming $100\%$-dead-end batches in NREM sleep requires the verifier to return a quantitative residual vector or directional correction $\Delta_{\text{verifier}}(\mathbf{x}_i, f_i) \ne \mathbf{0}$. When an external black-box evaluator returns only a binary crash bit ($0/1$) with zero stacktrace or margin signal, `FeedbackConditionedSelfTeacher` abstains ($\omega_i^{\text{SDPO}} = 0$ by the zero-residual gate, `abstained_zero_win = True`) and leaves slow-expert weights untouched while routing failures to contrastive `DAG-DPO` repulsion. Furthermore, when $\ge 50\%$ of verifier feedback vectors are adversarially corrupted in a coherent direction, median/MAD filtering reaches its theoretical breakdown point and relies on the `clean_anchor_xy` validation-loss fallback (Table 6B).

4. **Certified Local KKT Stationarity vs. Global Topological Optimality & High Algebraic Degree ($\deg P(\Delta_n^\star) > 78$ at $n=13, 14$):**
   Unlike $n=15$ (whose $D_3$-symmetric $12+3$ frame reduces via resultant elimination to a degree-5 minimal polynomial), LLL lattice reduction (`fmpz_mat.lll` in `python-flint`) at 4,800-digit precision proves that $\Delta_{13}^\star$ and $\Delta_{14}^\star$ have no minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$ of degree $d \le 78$ with coefficient height $\le 10^{60}$. Moreover, for $n \ge 7$, proving global optimality over all $\binom{n}{3}$ combinatorial triangle topologies remains an open problem in discrete geometry: our 500-digit KKT certificates and $10^{100}$-denominator exact rational floor embeddings certify exact KKT stationarity / local optimality on the active combinatorial topology and rigorous constructive lower bounds $\underline{\Delta}_n \le \Delta_n$ on the global constant.

5. **Broader Impact & Trustworthy Autonomous Discovery:**
   Unconstrained self-improving agents pose risks of *Grader Gaming* (fabricating intermediate branches or exploiting floating-point inaccuracies) and unsafe code execution. `SLEEP-DAG-RSI v8` mitigates these risks structurally via: (i) `AstPolicySafetyGate` (static AST whitelisting blocking `eval`, `exec`, `import`, dunder access, and filesystem/network calls); (ii) mandatory inverse projection $\Phi^{-1}$ into the base domain prior to external evaluation; (iii) the pre-committed hypothesis contract $\mathcal{H} = (c, \pi, d, a)$; and (iv) two-layer formal verification in `Lean 4` (`by native_decide`, zero `Float`, zero `sorryAx`).
