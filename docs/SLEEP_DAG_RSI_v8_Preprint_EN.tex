\begin{abstract}
Evolutionary code search and recursive self-improvement (RSI) frameworks powered by large language models (\emph{FunSearch}, \emph{AlphaEvolve}, \emph{Dream-RSI}, \emph{DiscoveryRSI}) and continual parametric consolidation architectures (\emph{SLEEP}, \emph{HOPE / Continuum Memory System}) have demonstrated strong results in isolated domains, yet suffer from structural failure modes when deployed over long horizons or non-smooth mathematical manifolds. First, we conduct a formal mathematical and forensic audit of baseline RSI architectures, proving that: (i) Equation (1) of \emph{Dream-RSI} exhibits a positive partial derivative with respect to wasted verifier probes ($\partial V_i^m / \partial N_i^m = -\beta_1 + \beta_2 / k_i^{m,\star} > 0$ whenever $k_i^{m,\star} < \beta_2/\beta_1$), actively rewarding policies for padding parallel batches with dummy evaluations; (ii) offline tree replay suffers from \emph{Support Censorship} and topological branch isolation; (iii) Equation (17) of \emph{DiscoveryRSI} (\texttt{arXiv:2610.23839v1}) assigns identical credit bonuses ($\lambda_p = 0.25$) to trivial syntax repairs and substantive breakthroughs, inducing a \emph{Cautious A-Student Collapse} where repair frequency inflates while novelty and importance degrade below the base model; and (iv) naive parametric sleep (\emph{Vanilla SLEEP}, \texttt{arXiv:2606.03979v2}) contaminates slow memory experts with unverified dead-end trajectories ($39.49\%$ trap contamination) and explodes under heavy-tailed input spikes.

To resolve these vulnerabilities within a single mathematically unified architecture, we introduce \textbf{Lean-DAG-RSI \& SLEEP-DAG-RSI (v1--v8)}. Our framework replaces isolated search trees with a \textbf{Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG)} governed by an \textbf{Extremal Max-Tilted Entropic Objective} ($J_\tau(\theta) = \frac{1}{\tau}\log \mathbb{E}[\exp(\tau R(y))]$, \emph{TTT-Discover} with upper-tail robust scale $s_R$), \textbf{Backward Sub-Goal Decomposition} of dead regime buckets (\emph{BES / LADDER}), \textbf{Error-Fingerprint \& Anchored Multi-Parent Submodular Hyper-Crossover}, and \textbf{Autonomous AST Code Splicing} (\texttt{AstCodeSplicer}) with leave-one-bucket-out (LOO) validated nonlinear routing predicate synthesis. \section{Introduction}

Recent progress in automated scientific discovery has been driven by coupling large language models (LLMs) with executable evaluators in evolutionary loops, including \emph{FunSearch} \cite{funsearch2024}, \emph{Evolution of Heuristics (EoH)} \cite{eoh2024}, \emph{ReEvo} \cite{reevo2024}, \emph{LLM-SR} \cite{llmsr2024}, \emph{AlgoTune} \cite{algotune2025}, \emph{AlphaEvolve v1/v2} \cite{alphaevolve2025,alphaevolve2_2025}, \emph{ThetaEvolve} \cite{thetaevolve2025}, \emph{TTT-Discover} \cite{tttdiscover2026}, \emph{Dream-RSI} \cite{dreamrsi2026}, and \emph{DiscoveryRSI} \cite{discoveryrsi2026}, extending classical symbolic regression and sparse dynamical discovery (\emph{SINDy} \cite{brunton2016}, \emph{AI Feynman} \cite{udrescu2020}, \emph{PySR} \cite{cranmer2020}). In parallel, continual parametric learning architectures such as \emph{EWC} \cite{kirkpatrick2017}, \emph{SLEEP} \cite{sleep2026}, \emph{HOPE / Continuum Memory System (CMS)} \cite{hope2025}, \emph{DeltaNet} \cite{yang2024}, and \emph{Gated DeltaNet} \cite{yang2025} have sought to endow neural networks with online fast-weight adaptation during ``Wake'' cycles and offline slow-weight consolidation during ``Sleep'' cycles.

Despite their conceptual appeal, deploying these systems on long-horizon discovery tasks reveals a fundamental disconnect between high-level algorithmic descriptions and the underlying optimization geometry:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Pathological Replay \& Credit Objectives:} As we prove in Section~\ref{sec:audit}, the counterfactual replay objective in Equation (1) of \emph{Dream-RSI} and the hypothesis credit weighting in Equation (17) of \emph{DiscoveryRSI} contain incentive inversions that actively reward dummy batch inflation and risk-averse syntax repairs over genuine structural crossovers.
\item \textbf{Tree Isolation \& Textual Blindness to Ill-Conditioned Manifolds:} Strictly tree-structured search graphs prevent recombining orthogonal breakthroughs discovered in sibling branches. Furthermore, when a discrete code structure depends on continuous parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ lying in a rotated epistatic valley ($\kappa_{\text{eff}}(G) \ge 10^8$) or a non-smooth minimax crease ($\min_{1 \le m \le M} A_m(\boldsymbol{\theta})$), random LLM text edits and naive first-order solvers stall completely.
\item \textbf{Black-Box Drift \& Dead-End Contamination in Neural Sleep:} Standard parametric consolidation (\emph{Vanilla SLEEP}) indiscriminately distills all wake trajectories into slow Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) weights, imprinting rejected dead-ends into long-term memory, while unnormalized fast associative memories (\emph{Hebbian / Delta-Rule}) diverge quadratically ($\propto \|\mathbf{x}_t\|_2^2$) under heavy-tailed sensor spikes. Conversely, pure DAG-based code masks ($\text{loss\_mask} = 0$ on failures) waste $100\%$ of compute whenever an exploratory round yields zero passing candidates.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Audit of Baseline RSI Architectures \& \texttt{Lean-DAG-RSI v1--v4}}
\label{sec:audit}

\subsection{Proposition 1: Probe-Spam Incentive Inversion in \emph{Dream-RSI} Equation (1)}

In Section 3 (p.~6, Eq.~(1)) of \emph{Dream-RSI} \cite{dreamrsi2026}, the counterfactual replay value of exploration policy $m$ on historical discovery tree $\mathcal{T}_i$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
V_i^m = \max_{v \in \mathcal{T}_i^{m, < k_i^{m,\star}}} s_v - \beta_1 N_i^m + \beta_2 \frac{N_i^m}{\max\{1, k_i^{m,\star}\}},
\tag{1}\label{eq:dreamrsi_eq1}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}_i^{m, < k_i^{m,\star}}$ is the revealed subtree up to stopping round $k_i^{m,\star} \ge 1$, $s_v$ is the verifier score of node $v$, $N_i^m$ is the total number of non-root nodes opened (verifier probes consumed), and $\beta_1, \beta_2 \ge 0$ are scalar weights penalizing total probes and rewarding average batch parallelism $N_i^m / k_i^{m,\star}$ (or equivalently under the min-max normalized objective $Q(V_i^m) = \alpha_1(\max_v s_v - s_{\text{root}}) - \beta_1 N_i^m + \beta_2 N_i^m / \max\{1, k_i^{m,\star}\}$ when $\alpha_2 = 0$).

\begin{proposition}[Positive Gradient for Wasted Parallel Probes]
\label{prop:probe_spam}
Let $\beta_2 > \beta_1 > 0$ (e.g., standard weights $\beta_1 = 0.01, \beta_2 = 0.05$). For any replay trajectory terminating in $k_i^{m,\star} < \beta_2 / \beta_1$ sequential rounds, the partial derivative of $V_i^m$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dreamrsi_eq1} with respect to the number of consumed verifier probes $N_i^m$ at fixed best score $\max_v s_v$ is strictly positive:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial V_i^m}{\partial N_i^m} = -\beta_1 + \frac{\beta_2}{k_i^{m,\star}} > 0 \qquad \forall\, k_i^{m,\star} \in \left\{1, \dots, \left\lceil \frac{\beta_2}{\beta_1} \right\rceil - 1\right\}.
\tag{2}\label{eq:probe_spam_grad}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:dreamrsi_eq1} with respect to $N_i^m$ while holding $\max_v s_v$ and $k_i^{m,\star} \ge 1$ constant yields $\partial V_i^m / \partial N_i^m = -\beta_1 + \beta_2 / k_i^{m,\star}$. Whenever $k_i^{m,\star} < \beta_2 / \beta_1$, we have $\beta_2 / k_i^{m,\star} > \beta_1$. In particular, at $k_i^{m,\star} = 1$ with $\beta_1 = 0.01, \beta_2 = 0.05$, appending $\Delta N$ completely useless dummy probes with zero score improvement ($\Delta \max s_v = 0$) strictly increases the objective by $+0.04\,\Delta N > 0$.
\end{proof}

Furthermore, forensic inspection of \emph{Dream-RSI} Appendix B.2 (\texttt{Listing 2}, \texttt{evaluate\_policy}) reveals that the reference code does not implement Eq.~\eqref{eq:dreamrsi_eq1} at all, optimizing instead \texttt{pareto.auc - lambda * (1.0 / mean\_parallelism)}, which similarly penalizes narrow batches and encourages redundant parallel probes.

\paragraph{Resolution in \texttt{Lean-DAG-RSI} (\texttt{engine.py:726}).}
We replace Eq.~\eqref{eq:dreamrsi_eq1} with a strictly monotone replay objective:
\begin{equation}
V_{\text{Lean}}(\mathcal{T}, \pi) = \max_{v \in \tau(\pi)} s_v - \beta_1 N_{\text{probes}}(\pi) - \beta_2 k_{\text{rounds}}(\pi), \qquad \frac{\partial V_{\text{Lean}}}{\partial N_{\text{probes}}} = -\beta_1 < 0, \quad \frac{\partial V_{\text{Lean}}}{\partial k_{\text{rounds}}} = -\beta_2 < 0.
\tag{3}\label{eq:v_lean}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Overcoming Support Censorship via Shift-Invariant Damped Tukey Continuation (\texttt{s\_cf})}

When an alternative policy $\pi'$ replays a fixed historical tree $\mathcal{T}$ and chooses to deepen a rapidly improving branch node $u$ ($\Delta_u = s(u) - s(\text{parent}(u)) > 0$) that was prematurely halted at depth $d_u < D_{\max}$ in the recorded log, standard replay returns a no-op ($\Delta_{\text{next}} = 0$). This \emph{Support Censorship} systematically penalizes policies that prioritize high-slope under-explored branches.

In \texttt{LeanDAGWorld} (\texttt{engine.py:678}), probing an unexpanded frontier node $u$ with $\Delta_u > 0$ spawns up to $m \in \{1, 2\}$ virtual counterfactual continuation steps with damped cumulative Tukey fencing:
\begin{equation}
s_{\text{cf}}(u, m) = s(u) + \left(\sum_{j=1}^m \gamma^j\right) \min\bigl(\Delta_u, C_{\text{Tukey}}(\Delta^+; \Delta_u)\bigr), \qquad \gamma = 0.55,
\tag{4}\label{eq:s_cf}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{Tukey}}(\Delta^+; \Delta_u) = (1 - \alpha_{\text{cf}})\,C_{\text{comp}}(\Delta_u) + \alpha_{\text{cf}}\bigl(Q_{75}(\Delta^+) + 1.5\,\operatorname{IQR}(\Delta^+)\bigr), \qquad C_{\text{comp}}(\Delta_u) = \frac{\Delta_u}{1 + \Delta_u / S_\Delta},
\tag{4a}\label{eq:s_cf_interp}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{\text{cf}} = \min\bigl(1,\, \max(0, (|\Delta^+| - 1)/3)\bigr) \in [0, 1]$ smoothly interpolates between the small-sample compression barrier ($|\Delta^+| = 1 \implies \alpha_{\text{cf}} = 0$) and the empirical interquartile fence ($|\Delta^+| \ge 4 \implies \alpha_{\text{cf}} = 1$), and $S_\Delta = \max\bigl(\operatorname{mean}(\Delta^+),\; |s(u) - s(\text{root})|,\; 10^{-6}\bigr)$ depends solely on score differences, guaranteeing exact \textbf{shift invariance} $s_{\text{cf}}(u, m; s + c) = s_{\text{cf}}(u, m; s) + c$ for any $c \in \mathbb{R}$ (\texttt{Suite 19h}). Across our 50-task synthetic benchmark (\texttt{lean\_dag\_rsi\_benchmark.py}), $V_{\text{Lean}} + s_{\text{cf}}$ defeats \emph{Dream-RSI} \texttt{OptimalPolicy} by \textbf{37 : 2} (11 ties) while reducing verifier calls by \textbf{30.6\%}.

\subsection{Error-Fingerprint DAG Crossover \& Anchored Multi-Parent Hyper-Crossover (\texttt{v2--v4})}

To break tree isolation without triggering combinatorial junk merges, every evaluated node $u$ records a binary pass mask $\mathbf{m}(u) \in \{0, 1\}^M$ (\texttt{pass\_mask}) and continuous normalized bucket margins $\boldsymbol{\phi}(u) \in [-1, 1]^M$ (\texttt{bucket\_margins}) across $M$ diagnostic regime slices, along with the set of modified AST symbols $\mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(u)$ (\texttt{ast\_touches} and line hotspots from nanosecond profiler \texttt{profile\_callable\_lines}).

Given the top-scoring anchor parent $u^\star = \arg\max_{u \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{succ}}} \bar{\phi}(u)$ and a candidate parent subset $S = \{u^\star, u_2, \dots, u_k\}$ ($k \ge 1$, anchored at the singleton seed $S_0 = \{u^\star\}$ where $\Delta_{\text{Hyper}}(S_0 \mid u^\star) = 0$), \texttt{compute\_hyper\_crossover} (\texttt{engine.py:2715}) defines the \textbf{Anchored Submodular Excess Margin Gain}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\text{Hyper}}(S \mid u^\star) = \underbrace{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M \max_{u \in S} \phi_j(u) - \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M \phi_j(u^\star)}_{f_{\text{cov}}(S) \text{ (monotone submodular)}} - \underbrace{\frac{\lambda_{\text{coll}}}{\max\!\bigl(1, \binom{|S|}{2}\bigr)} \sum_{\{u, v\} \subseteq S} \operatorname{IoU}\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(u), \mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(v)\bigr)}_{P_{\text{AST}}(S) \in [0, \lambda_{\text{coll}}] \text{ (pair-normalized AST collision penalty)}}.
\tag{5}\label{eq:hyper_xover}
\end{equation}
Anchoring the reference term at the fixed seed $u^\star \in S$ ensures that the coverage term $f_{\text{cov}}(S) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M \max_{u \in S} \phi_j(u) - \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M \phi_j(u^\star)$ is a genuine monotone non-negative submodular set function for all $S \supseteq \{u^\star\}$ ($f_{\text{cov}}(\{u^\star\}) = 0$), while the pair-normalized AST collision regularizer $P_{\text{AST}}(S)$ is a bounded non-submodular structural penalty in $[0, \lambda_{\text{coll}}]$ regardless of subset cardinality $|S|$ (so $\Delta_{\text{Hyper}}(S \mid u^\star) = f_{\text{cov}}(S) - P_{\text{AST}}(S)$ is a regularized submodular objective). Initialized at the singleton anchor $S_0 = \{u^\star\}$, greedy forward selection iteratively adds $w \notin S_t$ if and only if its penalized marginal gain satisfies $\Delta_{\text{Hyper}}(S_t \cup \{w\} \mid u^\star) - \Delta_{\text{Hyper}}(S_t \mid u^\star) > \tau_{\text{hyper}}$ ($\tau_{\text{hyper}} = 0.05$) with mean pairwise $\operatorname{IoU}(\mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(u), \mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(v)) < 0.5$.

\subsection{Scale-Invariant Gauss-Newton Epistatic Pruner (\texttt{v3--v4})}

When numerical solvers polish tunable continuous parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in [\mathbf{l}, \mathbf{u}] \subset \mathbb{R}^d$, over-parameterized models overfit training noise or drift along coupled epistatic ridges (e.g., $g(\theta_0, \theta_1) = \theta_0 / \theta_1$). We apply a two-stage regularizer:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Single-Coordinate Backward Elimination (\texttt{prune\_tunable\_params}, \texttt{engine.py:1609}):} Greedily resets coordinates $\theta_i^\star \to \theta_i^{(0)}$ whenever the cumulative drop from the unpruned optimum satisfies $f(\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star) - f(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{try}}) \le \delta_{\text{tol}}$.
\item \textbf{Scale-Invariant Epistatic Gauss-Newton Pruner (\texttt{prune\_epistatic\_gauss\_newton}, \texttt{engine.py:2989}):} Maps $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ to the dimensionless unit hypercube $\tilde{\theta}_i = (\theta_i - l_i)/(u_i - l_i) \in [0, 1]^d$ and constructs the positive semi-definite sensitivity matrix over $M$ buckets at macro-stencil $\delta = 0.04$ (chosen at $4\%$ of the unit box to step across micro-discretization jitter while capturing local curvature):
\begin{equation}
G_{\text{ep}} = \frac{1}{M} J(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^\star)^\top J(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^\star) + \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M \Bigl(H^{(j)}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^\star)\Bigr)^\top H^{(j)}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^\star) \succeq 0.
\tag{6}\label{eq:g_ep}
\end{equation}
Because $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \in [0, 1]^d$ is dimensionless and bucket margins $\phi_j \in [-1, 1]$ are normalized, both terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:g_ep} share compatible dimensionless units; moreover, at a local interior optimum where $J(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^\star) \approx \mathbf{0}$, the squared Hessian term $(H^{(j)})^\top H^{(j)} \succeq 0$ prevents false pruning of quadratically active ridges (\texttt{Suite 15}). Eigenvectors $\mathbf{q}_m$ with $\lambda_m \le 10^{-3}\lambda_{\max}$ loading on $\ge 2$ coordinates identify coupled epistatic ridges and freeze redundant degrees of freedom, followed by a 20-direction $\pm 5\%$ flat-plateau stress test (\texttt{evaluate\_flat\_plateau}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proposition 2: Credit Conservation \& Conjunctive Clone-Family Equivalence in \emph{DiscoveryRSI}}

In \emph{DiscoveryRSI} (\texttt{arXiv:2610.23839v1}, Appendix B.2, Eq.~(17), p.~27) \cite{discoveryrsi2026}, trajectory quality weights are computed as:
\begin{equation}
q_h = \min\Bigl(q_{\max},\; q_{\min} + (q_{\max} - q_{\min}) r_h \rho_{\text{cov}, h} \rho_{\text{unc}, h} + \lambda_p p_h\Bigr),
\tag{7}\label{eq:discoveryrsi_eq17}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_p = 0.25$ is identical for \texttt{necessary-repair} and \texttt{substantive-progress}. Because localized syntax repairs have lower evaluation variance $\sigma_h$, conservative TrueSkill ranking $s_h = \mu_h - 2\sigma_h$ systematically favors repairs over crossovers. Empirically (\emph{DiscoveryRSI} Fig.~6b \& 7a, p.~15), repair share inflates from $20\%$ to $28\%$, crossover share collapses to $3\%$, and out-of-distribution \texttt{Importance} ($6.48 \to 6.04$) and \texttt{Novelty} ($4.87 \to 4.58$) degrade below the unadapted base model.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded Clone-Family Credit Conservation under Conjunctive Equivalence]
\label{prop:credit_cap}
In \texttt{compute\_provenance\_credit} (\texttt{engine.py:2241}), let the progress bonus be split into $\lambda_{\text{breakthrough}} = 0.35 > \lambda_{\text{repair}} = 0.10$, let lineage passengers with zero verified diff receive weight $w_{\text{passenger}} = 0$, and define the \textbf{conjunctive clone-family equivalence relation} $h \sim_{\text{clone}} h'$ if and only if trajectories $h, h'$ share identical behavioral \texttt{pass\_mask} ($\mathbf{m}(h) = \mathbf{m}(h')$) \textbf{and} structural similarity $\operatorname{IoU}(\mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(h), \mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(h')) \ge 0.80$ (or identical normalized AST / parameter hash when \texttt{ast\_touches} is empty). Let each equivalence class $\mathcal{C}_f$ be normalized by local and global family caps $C_{\text{loc}} = C_{\text{glob}} = W_{\max} = 2.0$:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{w}_h = w_h \cdot \min\!\left(1.0,\; \frac{C_{\text{glob}}}{\sum_{h' \in \mathcal{C}_f} w_{h'}}\right).
\tag{8}\label{eq:credit_cap}
\end{equation}
Then: (i) for any number $K \ge 1$ of near-duplicate clones injected into $\mathcal{C}_f$, the total credit mass of $\mathcal{C}_f$ is strictly upper-bounded by $\sum_{h' \in \mathcal{C}_f} \tilde{w}_{h'} \le C_{\text{glob}} = 2.0$; (ii) algorithmically distinct solutions achieving the same \texttt{pass\_mask} via disjoint AST structures ($\operatorname{IoU} < 0.80$, e.g., Cholesky vs.\ Conjugate Gradient) occupy distinct equivalence classes $\mathcal{C}_f \ne \mathcal{C}_{f'}$ and suffer zero cross-family credit dilution; and (iii) breakthrough/crossover trajectories strictly dominate routine repairs of equal base rank.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Summing Eq.~\eqref{eq:credit_cap} over $h \in \mathcal{C}_f$ gives $\sum_{h \in \mathcal{C}_f} \tilde{w}_h = \min\bigl(\sum_{h \in \mathcal{C}_f} w_h,\, C_{\text{glob}}\bigr) \le C_{\text{glob}} = 2.0$ for all $K \ge 1$. Under the conjunctive condition, $\operatorname{IoU}(\mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(h), \mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(h')) < 0.80 \implies h \not\sim_{\text{clone}} h'$, preserving full credit for distinct algorithms. Finally, $q_{\text{breakthrough}} - q_{\text{repair}} = \lambda_{\text{breakthrough}} - \lambda_{\text{repair}} = 0.25 > 0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{\texttt{WaldMicroSentinel}: 5\% Multi-Fidelity Sequential UCB95 Early-Veto with Locked Stochastic Trimming}

Evaluating doomed candidate mutations across fine grids ($P = 20,256$) or large geometric constraint sets ($\binom{16}{3} = 560$ triangles) wastes $80\text{--}95\%$ of compute. Given a parent node $u$ with pointwise evaluation vector $\mathbf{e}_u \in \mathbb{R}^N$ (activated whenever $N \ge N_{\min} = 80$, so each stratum contains $\ge 2$ samples for unbiased variance estimation; for small grids $N < 80$, all $N$ points are evaluated directly):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Deterministic vs.\ Locked Stochastic Adversarial Core ($S_{\text{adv}}$) \& Canary Carving ($\le 5.0\%$ Single-Call Budget):} In deterministic tasks, $S_{\text{adv}}$ selects the $k = \lfloor 0.025 N \rfloor$ worst residual indices of parent $u$ and $S_{\text{strat}}$ selects $k = \lfloor 0.025 N \rfloor$ uniformly spaced indices disjoint from $S_{\text{adv}}$. In heavy-tailed stochastic dynamical systems ($\text{noise\_regime}=\texttt{"stochastic"}$), selecting the raw worst $2.5\%$ captures irreducible sensor spikes (flash crashes / Cauchy outliers), causing the optimizer to overfit noise. Therefore, \texttt{WaldMicroSentinel} trims the extreme top $q_{\text{trim}} = 5\%$ quantile (\texttt{parent\_trimmed\_indices}) \textbf{locked once on the parent node $u$} and selects $S_{\text{adv}}$ from the informative $92.5\%\text{--}95\%$ error band ($\text{adv\_spike\_overlap} = 0$). To enforce the hard $\le 5.0\%$ evaluation budget in a \textbf{single \texttt{eval\_slice\_fn} call} while still guarding against numerical explosions inside the locked parent trim region, stochastic mode carves $n_{\text{canary}} = \max(1, \lfloor 0.005 N \rfloor)$ ($0.5\%$) canary indices from the active slice (allocating $|S_{\text{adv}}| \approx 2.25\% N$, $|S_{\text{strat}}| \approx 2.25\% N$, and $|S_{\text{canary}}| \approx 0.5\% N$, totaling $|S_{\text{eval}}| \le \lfloor 0.05 N \rfloor$, $\text{cost\_fraction} \le 0.05$ with $\ge 2$ samples per stratum for all $N \ge 80$); any candidate whose raw error on $S_{\text{canary}}$ exceeds $\text{hard\_divergence\_ceiling} = 10^6$ is immediately vetoed (\texttt{Suite 19h}).
\item \textbf{Stratified Anchor ($S_{\text{strat}}$):} Uniformly spaced indices disjoint from $S_{\text{adv}}$ anchoring global scale ($S_{\text{adv}} \cap S_{\text{strat}} = \emptyset$).
\item \textbf{Sequential Upper-Confidence-Bound ($\operatorname{UCB}_{95}$) Early-Veto Test with Unbiased Stratified SE:} On $S_{\text{sentinel}} = S_{\text{adv}} \cup S_{\text{strat}}$, let $\Delta_i = s_v(i) - s_u(i)$ denote the pointwise score gain of candidate $v$ over parent $u$ (higher is better), with within-stratum sample variances $s_{\text{adv}}^2, s_{\text{strat}}^2$. We compute the \textbf{tail-risk-averse composite sentinel mean} $\hat{\mu}_\Delta = w_{\text{adv}}\bar{\Delta}_{\text{adv}} + w_{\text{strat}}\bar{\Delta}_{\text{strat}}$ (where $w_{\text{adv}} = |S_{\text{adv}}|/|S_{\text{sentinel}}| \approx 1/2$ and $w_{\text{strat}} = |S_{\text{strat}}|/|S_{\text{sentinel}}| \approx 1/2$ deliberately weight worst-case bottleneck performance equally with the global average to screen out candidates that regress the adversarial tail) and the \textbf{unbiased two-stratum standard error} $\operatorname{SE}_{\text{strat}}(\Delta)$, vetoing candidate $v$ whenever its Student-$t$-inflated $95\%$ \textbf{Upper Confidence Bound ($\operatorname{UCB}_{95}$)} is strictly negative:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{UCB}_{95}(\Delta) = \hat{\mu}_\Delta + \gamma_{\text{UCB}}\,c_t(\nu)\,\operatorname{SE}_{\text{strat}}(\Delta) < -\epsilon_{\text{tol}}, \qquad \operatorname{SE}_{\text{strat}}(\Delta) = \sqrt{w_{\text{adv}}^2 \frac{s_{\text{adv}}^2}{|S_{\text{adv}}|} + w_{\text{strat}}^2 \frac{s_{\text{strat}}^2}{|S_{\text{strat}}|}},
\tag{10}\label{eq:ucb95_sentinel}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_{\text{UCB}} = 1.96$ and $c_t(\nu) = 1 + 2.4/\nu + 3.5/\nu^2$ ($\nu = \max(1, \min(|S_{\text{adv}}|, |S_{\text{strat}}|) - 1)$ for small strata $< 30$, else $1.0$). When $\operatorname{UCB}_{95}(\Delta) < -\epsilon_{\text{tol}}$, evaluation aborts at $5\%$ cost, marking \texttt{node.fail\_class = "sentinel\_pruned"} ($\text{is\_success()} = \text{False}$) and exporting $(u \to v^+, v_{\text{aborted}})$ to \texttt{export\_dag\_dpo\_pairs}. Multi-parent crossovers and newly lifted coordinate frames are exempt from $S_{\text{adv}}$ and tested solely on $S_{\text{strat}}$. Across 10 random seeds on Heilbronn $n=16$ (\texttt{Trial 1}), \texttt{WaldMicroSentinel} reduces triangle evaluations by $\mathbf{76.44\% \pm 9.23\%}$ ($83.51\%$ canonical) with $\mathbf{100.0\%}$ ($10/10$) exact winner agreement.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proposition 3: Dual Gram Spectral Equivalence \& Numerical-Rank $\kappa_{\text{eff}}(G)$ in \texttt{SpectralFrameLifter}}

When optimizing $d$ parameters across $M$ regime buckets with Jacobian $J \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d}$ and primal Gauss-Newton matrix $G = \frac{1}{M}J^\top J \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, two classical pathologies arise when $M < d$ (e.g., $M = 6$ buckets and $d = 128$ or $2,560$ parameters):
(1) \emph{Rank-Deficient False Alarms:} Because $\operatorname{rank}(G) \le \min(M, d) < d$, the smallest $d - M$ eigenvalues of $G$ are identically zero ($\lambda_{\min}(G) = 0$), causing the naive condition number $\kappa(G) = \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min} \to \infty$ even on perfectly isotropic objectives; and
(2) \emph{Cubic $\mathcal{O}(d^3)$ Diagonalization Bottleneck:} Forming and diagonalizing $G \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ requires $\mathcal{O}(M d^2 + d^3)$ operations.

\begin{proposition}[Dual Gram Eigendecomposition \& Relative-Threshold Condition Number]
\label{prop:dual_gram}
Let $J \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times d}$ with $M < d$, and define the $M \times M$ Dual Gram matrix $K = \frac{1}{M} J J^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times M}$ (\texttt{\_dual\_gram\_eigh\_from\_jacobian}, \texttt{engine.py:3380}). Let $K = U \Lambda_M U^\top$ be the eigendecomposition of $K$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_M \ge 0$ and orthonormal eigenvectors $\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_M \in \mathbb{R}^M$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The non-zero spectrum of $G = \frac{1}{M}J^\top J \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ coincides identically with the non-zero spectrum of $K$, and for every $\lambda_r > 10^{-12}$, the vector
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{v}_r = \frac{1}{\sqrt{M \lambda_r}} J^\top \mathbf{u}_r \in \mathbb{R}^d
\tag{11}\label{eq:dual_eigvec}
\end{equation}
is an exact unit-norm eigenvector of $G$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_r$, computable in $\mathcal{O}(M^2 d + M^3)$ time instead of $\mathcal{O}(d^3)$.
\item Define the \textbf{relative-threshold numerical rank} $r_{\sup}(\varepsilon_{\text{null}}) = \bigl|\{m \in \{1, \dots, \min(M, d)\} : \lambda_m(G) > \varepsilon_{\text{null}}\lambda_{\max}(G)\}\bigr|$ with $\varepsilon_{\text{null}} = 10^{-12}$. Then the \textbf{Regularized Effective Condition Number} restricted to $r_{\sup}(\varepsilon_{\text{null}})$:
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\text{eff}}(G) = \frac{\lambda_{\max}(G)}{\max\bigl(\lambda_{r_{\sup}(\varepsilon_{\text{null}})}(G),\, 10^{-12}\bigr)}
\tag{12}\label{eq:kappa_eff}
\end{equation}
equals $1.0$ on any isotropic subspace of rank $M < d$ (zero false-positive triggers) while accurately detecting extreme multi-scale valleys ($\kappa_{\text{eff}} = 10^8$) even when a single dominant eigenvalue ($\lambda_1 = 1, \lambda_2 = \dots = \lambda_M = 10^{-8}$) causes the participation ratio $r_{\text{eff}} = (\operatorname{Tr} G)^2/\operatorname{Tr}(G^2) \approx 1.0$ to collapse (\texttt{Suite 19h}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Multiply $\mathbf{v}_r$ by $G$: $G \mathbf{v}_r = \frac{1}{M} J^\top J \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{M\lambda_r}} J^\top \mathbf{u}_r\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{M\lambda_r}} J^\top \left(\frac{1}{M} J J^\top \mathbf{u}_r\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{M\lambda_r}} J^\top (\lambda_r \mathbf{u}_r) = \lambda_r \mathbf{v}_r$. Orthonormality follows from $\mathbf{v}_r^\top \mathbf{v}_s = \frac{1}{M\sqrt{\lambda_r \lambda_s}} \mathbf{u}_r^\top (J J^\top) \mathbf{u}_s = \frac{\lambda_s}{\sqrt{\lambda_r \lambda_s}} \mathbf{u}_r^\top \mathbf{u}_s = \delta_{rs}$. Indexing the denominator at $r_{\sup}(\varepsilon_{\text{null}}) \le \min(M, d)$ excludes structural zero modes while retaining all active modes above $\varepsilon_{\text{null}}\lambda_{\max}$.
\end{proof}

When $\kappa_{\text{eff}}(G) \ge 10^6$, \texttt{SpectralFrameLifter} (\texttt{engine.py:3720}) queries \texttt{SpectralMetaAtlas} to apply one of three coordinate-frame transformations $\Phi \to \Phi'$ with mandatory exact inverse projection $\Phi^{-1}: \mathcal{X}' \to \mathcal{X}$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\texttt{EigenRotationTransform}}: Rotates into the stiff active eigen-subspace $\mathbf{y} = V_{\text{active}}^\top(\boldsymbol{\theta} - \boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{anchor}})$, applies active-mode rescaling $P = V_{\text{active}}\Lambda_{\text{active}}^{-1/2}$ so the preconditioned Hessian in lifted coordinates satisfies $\kappa(P^\top H P) = 1.0$, and freezes sloppy/null modes ($\lambda_r \le \varepsilon_{\text{null}}\lambda_{\max}$). Across 10 random 8D rotated valleys with $\kappa_{\text{eff}} = 10^8$ and $M=6 < d=8$ (\texttt{Trial 2}), this reaches $< 2.5\times 10^{-7}$ loss in $15$ steps ($1.18 \pm 0.04\text{ ms}$) vs.\ $7.1761 \pm 5.2062$ after $40$ steps in unlifted coordinates.
\item \textbf{\texttt{KktSlackTransform} with \texttt{condense\_active\_set}}: Lifts non-smooth minimax ties $\min_{1 \le i \le K} f_i(x)$ to $(x, z) \in \mathbb{R}^{d+K}$ and automatically prunes inactive slack variables ($f_i(x) - z_{\min} > \sigma_{\text{mult}}\hat{\sigma}_f$). At Heilbronn $n=16$, while the converged KKT optimum has exactly \textbf{24 tight equiareal triangles} ($M=24$, Section~\ref{sec:extremal}), \texttt{condense\_active\_set} with buffer multiplier $\sigma_{\text{mult}} = 0.05$ retains \textbf{57 near-active triangles} out of $560$ during pre-convergence wake steps (\texttt{Trial 1}) so triangles entering or leaving the active boundary are tracked without re-evaluating the 503 far-inactive triangles.
\item \textbf{\texttt{DyadicHomotopyTransform}}: Solves high-resolution grids on coarse dyadic levels ($P/2^k \to P$) with linear prolongation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texttt{AstCodeSplicer}: Structural Merging \& LOO-Validated Nonlinear OOS Input-Routing Synthesis}

In earlier evolutionary frameworks, \texttt{CROSSOVER(u, v)} merely prompted an LLM with two code strings. In \texttt{SLEEP-DAG-RSI v7}, \texttt{AstCodeSplicer} (\texttt{engine.py:3810}) parses parent Python ASTs via \texttt{ast.parse} and performs:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Structural AST Merge (\texttt{structural\_ast\_merge}) \& Multi-Parent Cascade ($|S| \ge 3$):} When parents modify disjoint helper functions and non-colliding intra-function variable assignments ($\operatorname{IoU}(\mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(u), \mathcal{A}_{\text{AST}}(v)) = 0$), their AST nodes are spliced directly into the base syntax tree. When \texttt{compute\_hyper\_crossover} selects $|S| = k \ge 3$ parents $S = \{u^\star, u_2, \dots, u_k\}$, \texttt{splice\_parents} executes a sequential left-associative pairwise reduction (\texttt{functools.reduce}), iteratively splicing the running hybrid $h_{m-1}$ ($h_1 = u^\star$) with the next complementary parent $u_m$ ($m = 2, \dots, k$) and synthesizing nested regime-routing branches when target expressions overlap.
\item \textbf{LOO-Validated Nonlinear OOS Input-Routing Predicate Synthesis (\texttt{\_synthesize\_input\_routing\_predicate}):} When two parents modify the same target expression but specialize in different input regimes (measured by bucket inputs $\mathbf{x}_b \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and parent bucket margins $\phi_b(u), \phi_b(v)$), \texttt{AstCodeSplicer} searches a 4-tier hierarchical candidate feature grammar ordered by Minimum Description Length (MDL) complexity $c_{\text{MDL}}(g) \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{G}_{\text{route}}(\mathbf{x}) = \Bigl\{x_c,\; |x_c|,\; \operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x}),\; x_i x_j,\; x_i^2 + x_j^2\Bigr\},
\tag{13}\label{eq:g_route}
\end{equation}
finding the simplest threshold split $g(\mathbf{x}) \ge \tau$ that maximizes the MDL-penalized routing margin gain:
\begin{equation}
J_{\text{route}}(g, \tau) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{b=1}^M \Bigl[\mathbb{I}\bigl(g(\mathbf{x}_b) \ge \tau\bigr)\phi_b(u) + \mathbb{I}\bigl(g(\mathbf{x}_b) < \tau\bigr)\phi_b(v)\Bigr] - \max\bigl(\bar{\phi}(u), \bar{\phi}(v)\bigr) - \lambda_{\text{MDL}}\,c_{\text{MDL}}(g).
\tag{13a}\label{eq:j_route}
\end{equation}
To prevent overfitting when $M \le 8$ buckets relative to $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ quadratic candidates in Tiers 3--4, \texttt{\_synthesize\_input\_routing\_predicate} enforces a \textbf{Leave-One-Bucket-Out (LOO) Cross-Validation Margin Check} ($\text{loo\_valid} = \text{True}$, requiring that for every held-out bucket $b$, the threshold $\tau^{(-b)}$ fit on the remaining $M-1$ buckets routes $\mathbf{x}_b$ to a parent with margin within $\varepsilon_{\text{LOO}} = 10^{-6}$ of the oracle optimum, \texttt{Suite 19h}), followed by \texttt{verify\_spliced\_epistasis} to guarantee that the spliced hybrid achieves $s(h) \ge \max(s(u), s(v)) - \varepsilon_{\text{tol}}$ with default tolerance $\varepsilon_{\text{tol}} = 10^{-4}$. This cracks both 1D half-space splits (\texttt{x[0] >= 0.0}) and bilinear XOR quadrant splits (\texttt{(x[0] * x[1]) >= 0.0}) with $\mathbf{100\%}$ OOS accuracy (\texttt{Suites 18d--18e, 19h}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{\texttt{NeuroSymbolicDistiller}, \texttt{SymbolicLibrary} \& Full-Target Multi-Cycle Re-Consolidation}

During NREM sleep, \texttt{NeuroSymbolicDistiller} (\texttt{sleep\_dag\_rsi.py:865}) extracts explicit Python AST equations from neural slow-expert residuals $\Delta f_m(X) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d_{\text{out}}}$ via pure-NumPy Coordinate-Descent Lasso + OLS refitting over a typed basis dictionary $\Psi(X) = \{x_i,\; x_i x_j,\; x_i^2,\; \tanh(x_i),\; \operatorname{sign}(x_i)\}$. To overcome the depth-2 ceiling of flat sparse regression (and standard \emph{SINDy} STLSQ \cite{brunton2016}), \texttt{SLEEP-DAG-RSI v7} introduces two mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{DreamCoder-Style Hierarchical \texttt{SymbolicLibrary} \& Outer-Projected Inner Gauss-Newton Search:}
Whenever a distilled multi-term expression achieves $R^2_{\text{OOS}} \ge 0.85$, it is abstracted into a reusable \texttt{LibraryPrimitive} $\operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x})$ storing its natural amplitude scale $s_k = \operatorname{std}(f_{\text{raw}}(X))$ and mean offset $\mu_k$ (so $f_{\text{raw}}(\mathbf{x}) = s_k \operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x}) + \mu_k$). Furthermore, if flat Lasso explains only partial variance ($0.25 \le R^2 < 0.92$) because the true physical law combines a saturating nonlinear core with additive outer linear terms $y = \tanh(\mathbf{w}_{\text{in}}^\top \boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x})) + \mathbf{w}_{\text{out}}^\top \boldsymbol{\psi}(\mathbf{x})$, \texttt{\_discover\_nonlinear\_inner\_submotif} first projects out the top active outer linear subspace $X_{\text{lin}}$ via the orthogonal complement residual $\mathbf{y}_{\text{nl}} = \bigl(I - X_{\text{lin}}(X_{\text{lin}}^\top X_{\text{lin}} + \lambda I)^{-1}X_{\text{lin}}^\top\bigr)\mathbf{y}$ (\texttt{Suite 19h}) and then executes 5 steps of damped Gauss-Newton with analytical Jacobian $J_{\tanh} = (1 - \tanh^2(z))\,\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbf{x})$ to recover $\mathbf{w}_{\text{in}}$ and register the inner argument as $\operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x})$. In subsequent NREM cycles, \texttt{SymbolicLibrary} injects nonlinear compositions $\tanh(s_k \operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x}) + \mu_k), \operatorname{sign}(\operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x})), \operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x}) \cdot x_i$, and $\operatorname{lib}_k(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \operatorname{sign}(x_i)$ into $\Psi(X)$.
\item \textbf{Full-Target Multi-Cycle Re-Consolidation ($\text{reconstruct\_uncorrupted\_target}=\text{True}$):}
A subtle mathematical trap occurs when distilling $y = \tanh(z) + 0.6 x_4$ across two sleep cycles: in Cycle 1, flat Lasso fits the best linear approximation $\hat{y}^{(1)} \approx c \cdot z + 0.6 x_4$ ($R^2 \approx 0.86$) and Residual-Keep subtracts it, leaving a \textbf{corrupted residual} $\Delta y^{\text{rem}} = \tanh(z) - c \cdot z$ that is no longer a clean $\tanh(z)$ function in Cycle 2. By setting $\text{reconstruct\_uncorrupted\_target}=\text{True}$, Cycle $c \ge 2$ reconstructs the uncorrupted target $Y_{\text{full}} = \Delta Y_{\text{rem}} + \sum_{t < c} f_{\text{AST}}^{(t)}(X)$ using \texttt{prior\_cycle\_callable}, re-distills $Y_{\text{full}}$ over the expanded \texttt{SymbolicLibrary} dictionary, \textbf{replaces} the prior-cycle AST callables $\{f_{\text{AST}}^{(t)}\}_{t < c}$ with the unified expression $f_{\text{AST}}^{(c)}$ (subtracting $f_{\text{AST}}^{(c)}(X)$ once from $Y_{\text{full}}$ so prior terms are never double-counted), and recovers the exact depth-3 law $y = \tanh(1.5 x_0 x_1 - 0.8\operatorname{sign}(x_2)) + 0.6 x_4$ with $\mathbf{R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 1.0000 \pm 0.0000}$ across all 10 seeds (\texttt{Trial 4}), whereas both flat \texttt{v6} distillation ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.0400 \pm 0.0717$) and \emph{SINDy} STLSQ ($R^2_{\text{OOS}} = 0.0251 \pm 0.0793$) fail completely.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{\texttt{ActiveBoundarySynthesizer}: Bichromatic Chord Bisection \& Committee Variance Ascent}

Passive replay buffers cluster inside smooth interior basins and rarely sample regime boundaries where candidate symbolic laws diverge from neural experts. In \texttt{SLEEP-DAG-RSI v7}, \texttt{ActiveBoundarySynthesizer} (\texttt{engine.py:4065}) actively synthesizes high-information boundary probes via a two-step procedure:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Bichromatic Chord Bisection:} Identifies pairs of anchor inputs $(\mathbf{x}_a, \mathbf{x}_b)$ where committee models $f_1, \dots, f_K$ (e.g., neural fast memory vs.\ slow MoE expert vs.\ candidate AST) exhibit opposite residual signs, and performs $T_{\text{bisect}} = 8$ steps of binary chord bisection to isolate exact decision-boundary crossings.
\item \textbf{Projected Committee Variance Ascent:} Executes projected gradient ascent on the ensemble variance $\mathcal{V}(\mathbf{x}) = \operatorname{Var}_{1 \le k \le K}\bigl(f_k(\mathbf{x})\bigr)$ within a local trust ball $\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{anc}}\|_2 \le r_{\text{trust}}$. Across \texttt{Suite 18b} and \texttt{Trial 4}, this amplifies committee disagreement by $\mathbf{11.00\times\text{--}20.23\times}$ over passive replay anchors ($0.1423 \to 2.8785$) and pinpoints exact sign-flipping counterexamples for NREM distillation.
\end{enumerate}

\label{sec:v8}

\subsection{\texttt{TTT-Discover} Extremal Max-Tilted Objective (\texttt{compute\_max\_tilted\_objective})}

Standard RL and evolutionary portfolios optimize the expected reward $\mathbb{E}[R(y)]$, which favors high-probability mediocre variations over rare, high-variance extremal breakthroughs. Inspired by \emph{TTT-Discover} \cite{tttdiscover2026,sun2024}, we introduce the scale-normalized \textbf{Entropic Max-Tilted Objective} (\texttt{engine.py:4295} and \texttt{sleep\_dag\_rsi.py:1348}):
\begin{equation}
J_\tau(R; \theta) = R_{\max} + \frac{\operatorname{stop\_grad}(s_R)}{\tau} \log \left(\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^K \exp\!\left(\tau \frac{R_i - R_{\max}}{\operatorname{stop\_grad}(s_R)}\right)\right) - \beta_{\text{KL}}\,D_{\text{KL}}\bigl(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{\text{ref}}\bigr),
\tag{14}\label{eq:max_tilt}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
w_i(\tau) = \frac{\exp\bigl(\tau (R_i - R_{\max})/\operatorname{stop\_grad}(s_R)\bigr)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \exp\bigl(\tau (R_j - R_{\max})/\operatorname{stop\_grad}(s_R)\bigr)}, \qquad s_R = \max\!\Bigl(\operatorname{std}\bigl(\{R_i : R_i \ge \operatorname{median}(R)\}\bigr),\; 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}(R),\; 10^{-6}\Bigr).
\tag{14a}\label{eq:max_tilt_weights}
\end{equation}
Computing $s_R$ from the \textbf{upper-tail dispersion above the median} (with a detached $\operatorname{stop\_grad}(s_R)$ flag) ensures that a single catastrophic crash ($R_i = -10^6$) cannot blow up $s_R$ and collapse the effective inverse temperature $\tau / s_R \to 0$ (\texttt{Suite 19h}).

\begin{proposition}[Monotonic Convergence to the Extremum]
\label{prop:max_tilt}
For any finite sample of rewards $R = (R_1, \dots, R_K) \in \mathbb{R}^K$ and fixed positive scale $s_R > 0$, the tilted expectation $E_\tau(R) = \sum_{i=1}^K w_i(\tau) R_i$ and entropic objective $J_\tau(R)$ (at $\beta_{\text{KL}}=0$) are monotonically non-decreasing in $\tau \ge 0$, with $\frac{\partial E_\tau(R)}{\partial \tau} = \frac{1}{s_R}\operatorname{Var}_{w(\tau)}(R) \ge 0$, $\lim_{\tau \to 0^+} J_\tau(R) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^K R_i$, and $\lim_{\tau \to \infty} J_\tau(R) = \lim_{\tau \to \infty} E_\tau(R) = \max_{1 \le i \le K} R_i$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiating $E_\tau(R) = \sum_{i=1}^K w_i(\tau) R_i$ with respect to $\tau$ yields $\frac{1}{s_R}\bigl(\sum_i w_i(\tau) R_i^2 - (\sum_i w_i(\tau) R_i)^2\bigr) = \frac{1}{s_R}\operatorname{Var}_{w(\tau)}(R) \ge 0$. As $\tau \to \infty$, all exponential terms with $R_i < R_{\max}$ vanish at rate $\exp(-\tau(R_{\max} - R_i)/s_R)$, concentrating full probability mass on $\arg\max_i R_i$.
\end{proof}

In \texttt{LeanDAGPortfolio.rank\_parents\_max\_tilted}, parent selection weights branches by $w_i(\tau)$, while in \texttt{UniversalNeuralSleepAdapter.nrem\_consolidate(use\_max\_tilted\_consolidation=True, entropic\_tau=4.0)}, NREM gradient steps weight winning trajectories by $w_i(\tau)$, concentrating $\mathbf{0.9994\text{--}1.0000}$ of consolidation mass on the true breakthrough branch (\texttt{Trial 5} \& \texttt{Suites 19a, 19g, 19h}).

\subsection{\texttt{BES / LADDER} Backward Sub-Goal Decomposition (\texttt{BackwardSubGoalDecomposer})}

When a hard evaluation regime bucket $b^\star \in \{0, \dots, M-1\}$ is a \textbf{dead bucket} ($\max_{u \in \mathcal{T}} m_{b^\star}(u) = 0$, i.e., every explored branch has negative margin $\phi_{b^\star}(u) \le 0$), column $b^\star$ of the \texttt{pass\_mask} matrix is identically zero and \texttt{compute\_hyper\_crossover} cannot discover a synergy bridge. In continuous optimization tasks, $\phi_b(u) = (\tau_b - \ell_b(u))/|\tau_b| \in [-1, 1]$ is the normalized threshold deficit; in discrete code synthesis test suites with binary pass/fail outcomes, $\phi_b(u) \in [-1, 0)$ is defined via the dense execution surrogate margin (fraction of passed sub-assertions minus $1$, negative normalized stacktrace depth, or continuous output distance prior to binary thresholding). Combining \emph{Backward Exploratory Search (BES)} \cite{bes2025} and \emph{LADDER} \cite{ladder2025}, \texttt{BackwardSubGoalDecomposer} (\texttt{engine.py:4385}) identifies the closest near-miss anchor node $u_{\text{anc}}^\star = \arg\max_u \phi_{b^\star}(u) < 0$ and synthesizes a \textbf{3-rung stepping-stone ladder} with relaxed margin thresholds $\tau_{b^\star}^{(k)} = \alpha_k \phi_{b^\star}(u_{\text{anc}}^\star)$ ($\alpha_k \in \{0.5, 0.25, 0.0\}$), coarser problem scale $N^{(k)} = \max(8, \lfloor N / 2^k \rfloor)$, and relaxed tolerance $\epsilon^{(k)} = 10^k \epsilon$. Any candidate clearing the first rung $\phi_{b^\star}(v) \ge \tau_{b^\star}^{(1)}$ is promoted by \texttt{select\_stepping\_stone\_seed} as the root seed \texttt{promoted\_root\_seed\_id} for the next round (\texttt{Suite 19b}).

\subsection{\texttt{SDPO} Feedback-Conditioned Self-Teacher on 100\%-Dead-End Batches (\texttt{FeedbackConditionedSelfTeacher})}

In \texttt{SLEEP-DAG-RSI v5}, when an exploratory Wake batch on a hard domain yields $100\%$ dead-ends ($\sum_i m_{\text{win}}(u_i) = 0$), \texttt{DAG-KS} sets $\text{loss\_mask} = 0$ everywhere, wasting the entire NREM cycle. Inspired by \emph{Self-Distillation with Policy Optimization (SDPO)} \cite{sdpo2026}, \texttt{FeedbackConditionedSelfTeacher} (\texttt{engine.py:4515}, \texttt{sleep\_dag\_rsi.py:1128}) turns rich verifier diagnostics $\Delta_{\text{verifier}}(\mathbf{x}_i, f_i)$ on failed attempts $\mathbf{y}_i^{\text{attempt}}$ into corrected self-teacher targets:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{y}}_i^{\text{teacher}} = \mathbf{y}_i^{\text{attempt}} + \gamma_i \cdot \Delta_{\text{verifier}}(\mathbf{x}_i, f_i), \qquad \omega_i^{\text{SDPO}} = \omega_{\max} \cdot w_{\text{Tukey}}(r_i) \cdot \frac{r_i}{r_i + \sigma_{\text{med}}},
\tag{15}\label{eq:sdpo}
\end{equation}
where $r_i = \|\Delta_{\text{verifier}, i}\|_2$. To protect NREM consolidation against identical-assertion ties, non-convex kinks, and adversarial verifier corruption up to and beyond the $50\%$ breakdown point, we enforce four safeguards:
(1) \emph{Zero-Residual Abstention Gate ($\text{abstained\_zero\_win} = \text{True}$):} Attempts with $r_i \le 10^{-8}$ receive $\omega_i^{\text{SDPO}} = 0$, and whenever $\text{direct\_win\_count} == 0$ and $\text{sdpo\_reclaimed\_count} == 0$, \texttt{nrem\_consolidate} abstains completely ($\text{sample\_weights} = 0$) so slow-expert weights remain strictly untouched (\texttt{Suites 19g, 19h});
(2) \emph{MAD Floor Guard \& Axial/Directional Cosine Filter:} Given median residual norm $m_r = \operatorname{median}(r)$ and $\operatorname{MAD}_r = \operatorname{median}(|r - m_r|)$, we enforce the scale floor $\sigma_{\text{MAD}} = \max(1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}_r,\; 0.15\,m_r,\; 10^{-8})$ to prevent $0/0$ rejection when $\ge 50\%$ of dead-end attempts fail on the exact same assertion ($\operatorname{MAD}_r = 0$, \texttt{Suite 19h}), rejecting any sample with $z_i = |r_i - m_r| / \sigma_{\text{MAD}} > c_{\text{MAD}}$ or misaligned cosine similarity;
(3) \emph{Principal-Axis Bilateral Crease Mode:} When failed attempts straddle a non-smooth V-shaped cliff ($y^\star(\mathbf{x}) = -|\mathbf{x}|$), opposing gradient slopes across the crease cancel out ($\|\bar{\mathbf{g}}\|_2 \approx 0$). \texttt{reclaim\_vector\_dead\_ends} extracts the principal singular axis $\mathbf{u}_1(\Delta)$ of the verifier residual matrix, partitions inliers into $I_+ = \{i : \langle \Delta_i, \mathbf{u}_1 \rangle \ge 0\}$ and $I_- = \{i : \langle \Delta_i, \mathbf{u}_1 \rangle < 0\}$ (centering around the coordinate-wise median when shifted), and checks whether both clusters are balanced ($\min(|I_+|, |I_-|)/N \ge 0.20$), anti-parallel ($\cos(\bar{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_+, \bar{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_-) < -0.50$), and explained by the bilateral piecewise-linear crease basis with $R^2_{\text{crease}} \ge 0.85$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{y}}_i^{\text{crease}}(\mathbf{x}_i) = W_1 \mathbf{x}_i + W_2 |\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{c}| + \mathbf{b}, \qquad R^2_{\text{crease}} \ge 0.85 \implies \text{Bilateral Crease Mode} \quad (\text{axial } |\cos(\Delta_i, \mathbf{u}_1)|),
\tag{15a}\label{eq:sdpo_crease}
\end{equation}
smoothing reconciled targets via $(1-\eta_c)(\mathbf{y}_i + \gamma_i \Delta_i) + \eta_c \hat{\mathbf{y}}_i^{\text{crease}}(\mathbf{x}_i)$ ($\eta_c = 0.35$, \texttt{Suite 19h}); and
(4) \emph{Validation-Anchor Safety Fallback (\texttt{clean\_anchor\_xy}):} Whenever a clean validation anchor is provided and the proposed update would increase anchor loss (at $\ge 50\%$ corruption), the teacher triggers $\text{fallback\_triggered} = \text{True}$, abstains from slow-expert updates ($\text{abstained\_zero\_win} = \text{True}$), and preserves pre-update weights (\texttt{Trial 6}, Table~\ref{tab:trial6_ablations}).

\subsection{\texttt{MutableSkillState} (\texttt{SKILL.state}): Fixed-Capacity Cross-Cycle Memory}

Appending raw round traces to LLM prompts causes $\mathcal{O}(T)$ context bloat and attention dilution over long horizons. \texttt{MutableSkillState} (\texttt{engine.py:4605}, \texttt{sleep\_dag\_rsi.py:1395}) maintains a schema-validated, bounded JSON state updated at the end of each cycle via \texttt{commit\_cycle(tree)} with configurable capacity caps (compact prompt profile / extended orchestrator profile):
(i) \texttt{confirmed\_invariants} ($\le K_{\text{inv}} \in \{8, 16\}$): top verified parameter/structural invariants sorted by score gain;
(ii) \texttt{dead\_end\_fingerprints} ($\le K_{\text{dead}} = 16$): deduplicated by \texttt{(fail\_class, error\_signature)} with cumulative \texttt{occurrences} counters;
(iii) \texttt{unsolved\_buckets} ($\le K_{\text{buck}} \in \{8, 32\}$): tracks active deficit margins and \textbf{automatically evicts} any bucket $b$ as soon as a node achieves $\phi_b(u) > 0$; and
(iv) \texttt{active\_primitives} ($\le K_{\text{prim}} \in \{8, 12\}$): reusable AST functions from \texttt{SymbolicLibrary}.
All floats pass through an RFC-8259 sanitizer (\texttt{\_sanitize\_json\_float}), guaranteeing strict JSON serializability ($\text{allow\_nan}=\text{False}$). Over 50 discovery rounds (\texttt{Trial 5}), \texttt{SKILL.state} requires $\mathbf{1,348}$ bytes versus $74,820$ bytes for an append-only log ($\mathbf{-98.20\%}$ compression).

\subsection{\texttt{VeriHarness} Dual-Head Verification: \texttt{Disagreement Resolver} + Smooth Signed-Margin \texttt{Consensus Challenger}}

Standard active learning (\texttt{ActiveBoundarySynthesizer} in \texttt{v7}) searches for points where committee models $f_1, \dots, f_K$ disagree ($\max_{\mathbf{x}} \operatorname{Var}_k(f_k(\mathbf{x}))$). However, when all committee members share the same architectural inductive bias, they suffer from \textbf{unanimous blind spots}: $\operatorname{Var}_k(f_k(\mathbf{x})) \approx 0$ even right next to a decision cliff where the entire ensemble is wrong.

Inspired by \emph{VeriHarness} \cite{veriharness2026}, \texttt{synthesize\_dual\_head\_verification} (\texttt{engine.py:4205}) pairs \textbf{Head A (\texttt{Disagreement Resolver})} with \textbf{Head B (\texttt{Consensus Challenger})}, which performs projected gradient descent within trust ball $\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{anc}}\|_2 \le r_{\text{trust}}$ to \textbf{minimize the smooth signed consensus margin}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}_{\text{cons}}(\mathbf{x}) = s_{\text{anc}} \cdot \bar{f}(\mathbf{x}) + \lambda_{\text{var}}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_k\bigl(f_k(\mathbf{x})\bigr) + \epsilon}, \qquad s_{\text{anc}} = \operatorname{sign}\bigl(\bar{f}(\mathbf{x}_{\text{anc}})\bigr) \in \{-1, +1\}, \quad \bar{f}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K f_k(\mathbf{x}).
\tag{16}\label{eq:veriharness}
\end{equation}
Because $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{M}_{\text{cons}}(\mathbf{x}) = s_{\text{anc}}\nabla\bar{f}(\mathbf{x}) + \frac{\lambda_{\text{var}}}{2\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_k + \epsilon}}\nabla\operatorname{Var}_k(\mathbf{x})$ is smooth and non-vanishing even when $\operatorname{Var}_k(f_k(\mathbf{x})) = 0$ identically, descending $-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\mathcal{M}_{\text{cons}}$ pushes $\mathbf{x}$ directly across the zero-level set into the negative signed-margin region ($s_{\text{anc}}\bar{f}(\mathbf{x}) < 0$) where all committee models flip sign in unison, certified against an optional \texttt{ground\_truth\_oracle\_fn} (\texttt{Suites 19e, 19h}).

\subsection{\texttt{FALCON-2} Prefix-Aligned NLMS Fast-Weight Adaptation vs.\ \texttt{Gated DeltaNet}}

In \texttt{DeltaRuleFastMemory} (\texttt{v5}), the unnormalized outer-product update $\Delta M_t = \eta_t (\mathbf{v}_t - M_{t-1}\mathbf{k}_t)\mathbf{k}_t^\top$ scales quadratically with input norm ($\propto \|\mathbf{x}_t\|_2^2$). A single $10^3\times$ sensor spike causes immediate numerical overflow ($\text{loss} = 8.18\times 10^{14}$). Furthermore, naive prefix mixing $\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_t = (1-\alpha)\mathbf{k}_t + \alpha \mathbf{k}_{t-1}$ (used in standard \emph{Gated DeltaNet} \cite{yang2025}) suffers from catastrophic norm cancellation when an opposite-sign spike arrives ($\mathbf{x}_t \approx -\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\mathbf{x}_{t-1} \implies \|\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_t\|_2 \to 0$).

Inspired by \emph{FALCON-2} \cite{falcon2_2026}, we implement \textbf{Prefix-Aligned Normalized Least-Mean-Squares (NLMS)} with a \textbf{running prefix average of unit-normalized keys} $\bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t-1}$, cosine alignment gate, max-norm denominator, and \textbf{Huber innovation clipping} (\texttt{sleep\_dag\_rsi.py:406} and \texttt{2202}, with default hyperparameters $\eta_t = 0.05$, $\lambda_{\text{NLMS}} = 1.0$, $\alpha_{\min} = 0.10$, and Huber threshold $c_{\text{innov}} = 10.0$):
\begin{equation}
\Delta M_t = \frac{\eta_t \, \alpha_{\text{prefix}}(t)}{\max\bigl(\|\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_t\|_2^2,\, \|\mathbf{k}_t\|_2^2\bigr) + \lambda_{\text{NLMS}}} \,\operatorname{clip}_{\text{Huber}}\bigl(\mathbf{v}_t - M_{t-1}\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_t,\; c_{\text{innov}}\bigr)\,\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_t^\top, \qquad \alpha_{\text{prefix}}(t) = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{1 + \cos(\hat{\mathbf{k}}_t, \bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t-1})}{2},\; \alpha_{\min},\; 1.0\right),
\tag{17}\label{eq:falcon2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{k}}_t = \frac{\mathbf{k}_t}{\|\mathbf{k}_t\|_2 + 10^{-12}}, \qquad \bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t} = \frac{t - 1}{t}\,\bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t-1} + \frac{1}{t}\,\hat{\mathbf{k}}_t, \qquad \tilde{\mathbf{k}}_t = \bigl(1 - \alpha_{\text{prefix}}(t)\bigr)\mathbf{k}_t + \alpha_{\text{prefix}}(t)\,\|\mathbf{k}_t\|_2\,\frac{\bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t-1}}{\|\bar{\mathbf{k}}_{1:t-1}\|_2 + 10^{-12}}.
\tag{17a}\label{eq:falcon2_prefix}
\end{equation}
Across 10 random seeds (\texttt{Trial 6}, Table~\ref{tab:trial6_ablations}), \texttt{FALCON-2} achieves $\mathbf{13.5\times}$ lower post-spike loss than \emph{Gated DeltaNet} under opposite-sign $[1000\mathbf{v}, -333.3\mathbf{v}]$ cancellation spikes ($41,816.21$ vs.\ $564,478.28$) and $\mathbf{101.9\times}$ lower fast-weight blowup under $10^3\times$ target outliers ($\|B_{\text{fast}}\|_F = 3.25$ vs.\ $331.04$).

\subsection{Theorem 1: Resolution of the 1D Equiareal Tie-System Trap at Heilbronn $n = 13$ and $n = 16$}

In Heilbronn's triangle problem \cite{cantrell2007,stead2026}, $n$ points $\mathbf{p}_0, \dots, \mathbf{p}_{n-1}$ are placed in a unit-area triangle $T$ to maximize $\Delta_n = \min_{0 \le i < j < k < n} \operatorname{Area}(\mathbf{p}_i, \mathbf{p}_j, \mathbf{p}_k)$. Because global optimality over all $\binom{n}{3}$ combinatorial triangle topologies remains open for $n \ge 7$, numerical discovery seeks \textbf{certified local KKT stationary optima} $\Delta_n^\star$ on the best discovered active combinatorial topology, paired with exact $10^{100}$-denominator rational vertex roundings $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i \in \mathbb{Q}^2 \cap T$ certifying rigorous constructive lower bounds $\underline{\Delta}_n = \min_{i<j<k}\operatorname{Area}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i, \hat{\mathbf{p}}_j, \hat{\mathbf{p}}_k) \le \Delta_n$ ($|\Delta_n^\star - \underline{\Delta}_n| < 10^{-99}$). Prior records at $n=13$ (\texttt{0.026556528919738264474389245471}, Tej Stead, July 2026, $M=20$ active triangles) and $n=16$ (\texttt{0.0183555463554034501359529610459}, Fable 5.1 \& Shengtong Zhang, Sept 2026, $M=24$ active triangles) were refined to 30--80 digits by solving the area-equality tie system $A_1(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = A_2(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \dots = A_M(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ via damped Gauss-Newton, assuming the tie system defines an isolated 0-dimensional point.

\begin{theorem}[Corank-1 Equiareal Manifold $\Gamma(s)$ and Bordered KKT-Newton Resolution at $n=13$ and $n=16$]
\label{thm:heilbronn_corank1}
Let $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{free}}}$ denote the free vertex coordinates of the active frame after fixing the 6 boundary constraints (2 vertices on each triangle side, all of whose outward normal KKT multipliers are strictly dual-feasible $\mu_{\text{boundary}, r}^\star > 0$):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{At $n = 16$ ($15 + 1$ Floater Split):} Exactly 15 vertices form the active frame ($d_{\text{free}} = 2\times 15 - 6 = 24$) carrying all $M = 24$ minimal triangles, while the 16th vertex (\texttt{p\_13 = (0.440423714855, 0.491088435790)}, corresponding to \texttt{vertex 1} in \texttt{math.tejstead.com/heilbronn/triangle/16/}) is a strict interior floater participating in \textbf{zero} active triangles with Voronoi cell clearance $\mathbf{0.0185320014278944} > \Delta_{16}^\star + 1.7645 \times 10^{-4}$.
\item \textbf{Corank-1 Equiareal Curve $\Gamma(s)$:} In both $n=13$ ($d_{\text{free}} = 20, M = 20$) and $n=16$ ($d_{\text{free}} = 24, M = 24$), the $M \times d_{\text{free}}$ active area Jacobian $J = \frac{\partial(A_1, \dots, A_M)}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}}$ (with $m$-th row $J_m = \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} A_m(\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d_{\text{free}}}$) at the KKT stationary point $\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star$ has rank $d_{\text{free}} - 1$ ($\operatorname{rank}(J_{13}) = 19 < 20$, $\|J_{13}\mathbf{v}_0\|_2 = 1.03\times 10^{-551}$; and $\operatorname{rank}(J_{16,\text{frame}}) = 23 < 24$, $\|J_{16,\text{frame}}\mathbf{v}_0\|_2 = 9.08\times 10^{-552}$). Consequently, the $M-1$ tie-system equations $A_m(\boldsymbol{\theta}) - A_1(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = 0$ define a \textbf{smooth 1-dimensional equiareal curve} $\Gamma(s) = \boldsymbol{\theta}^\star + s\mathbf{v}_0 + s^2\mathbf{w} + \mathcal{O}(s^3)$ along the unit right nullvector $\mathbf{v}_0 \in \ker J$ on which all $M$ triangles have strictly equal area $\Delta(s)$ to arbitrary precision.
\item \textbf{Parameterization-Independent KKT Curvature $c > 0$ and Bordered KKT-Newton System:} Because $\ker(J^\top)$ is 1-dimensional and spanned by strictly positive KKT multipliers $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star > 0$ ($\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^\star = 1$, $J^\top \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star = \mathbf{0}$), multiplying the second-order Taylor expansion $A_m(\boldsymbol{\theta}(s)) = \Delta^\star + s J_m \mathbf{v}_0 + s^2 \left(J_m \mathbf{w} + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{v}_0^\top \nabla^2 A_m \mathbf{v}_0\right) + \mathcal{O}(s^3)$ by $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\star\top}$ annihilates the normal correction $\mathbf{w}$ identically ($\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\star\top} J \mathbf{w} = 0$), yielding:
\begin{equation}
\Delta(s) = \Delta^\star - c \cdot s^2 + \mathcal{O}(s^3), \qquad c = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m^\star \,\mathbf{v}_0^\top \nabla^2 A_m(\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star)\mathbf{v}_0 > 0,
\tag{18}\label{eq:kkt_curvature}
\end{equation}
with certified values $c_{13} = \mathbf{0.036078780237794575\dots} > 0$ and $c_{16} = \mathbf{0.011234563953840429\dots} > 0$. Running damped Gauss-Newton on the tie system from a \texttt{float64} iterate at arc-length offset $s_0 \sim 10^{-6}$ projects orthogonally onto $\Gamma(s)$ at $s \approx s_0 \ne 0$ and terminates immediately with zero tie residual at a value $c s_0^2$ below the apex. To converge quadratically to the exact apex $s = 0$, we solve the $(d_{\text{free}} + M + 1) \times (d_{\text{free}} + M + 1)$ \textbf{bordered KKT-Newton system} for $(\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}, \delta\boldsymbol{\lambda}, \delta\Delta) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{free}}} \times \mathbb{R}^M \times \mathbb{R}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix} H_{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) & J(\boldsymbol{\theta})^\top & \mathbf{0} \\ J(\boldsymbol{\theta}) & \mathbf{0} & -\mathbf{1}_M \\ \mathbf{0}^\top & \mathbf{1}_M^\top & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \delta\boldsymbol{\theta} \\ \delta\boldsymbol{\lambda} \\ \delta\Delta \end{bmatrix} = -\begin{bmatrix} J(\boldsymbol{\theta})^\top \boldsymbol{\lambda} \\ \mathbf{A}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) - \Delta \mathbf{1}_M \\ \mathbf{1}_M^\top \boldsymbol{\lambda} - 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad H_{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) = \sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m \nabla^2_{\boldsymbol{\theta}\boldsymbol{\theta}} A_m(\boldsymbol{\theta}).
\tag{18a}\label{eq:bordered_kkt}
\end{equation}
Because $\mathbf{v}_0^\top H_{\mathcal{L}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star, \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star) \mathbf{v}_0 = -2c < 0$ is strictly negative definite on $\ker J(\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star) = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{v}_0)$ and $\mathbf{1}_M^\top \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star = 1 \ne 0$ spans $\ker(J(\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star)^\top)$, the bordered KKT matrix in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bordered_kkt} is \textbf{strictly nonsingular} at $(\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star, \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star, \Delta^\star)$, yielding quadratic convergence to 560 decimal digits:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{13}^\star = \mathbf{0.02655652891977558013836441122594677335105195594906545441276197643\dots} \quad (\mathbf{+3.731566 \times 10^{-14}} \text{ at } s_0 = 1.0170 \times 10^{-6}),
\tag{18b}\label{eq:heilbronn_n13}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{16}^\star = \mathbf{0.01835554635546114041232536365340313429459832905287243778082350191\dots} \quad (\mathbf{+5.769028 \times 10^{-14}} \text{ at } s_0 = 2.2661 \times 10^{-6}).
\tag{18c}\label{eq:heilbronn_n16}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $L = [-\mathbf{1}_{M-1},\; I_{M-1}] \in \mathbb{R}^{(M-1)\times M}$ denote the tie-difference operator. Since $\ker(J^\top) = \operatorname{span}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star)$ with $\mathbf{1}_M^\top\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star = 1 \ne 0$ and $\ker(L^\top) = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{1}_M)$, any $\mathbf{u} \in \ker((LJ)^\top)$ satisfies $L^\top\mathbf{u} = \alpha\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star \implies 0 = (\mathbf{1}_M^\top L^\top)\mathbf{u} = \alpha(\mathbf{1}_M^\top\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star) = \alpha \implies \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0}$, proving $\operatorname{rank}(LJ) = M-1$. By the Implicit Function Theorem, $L\mathbf{A}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \mathbf{0}$ defines a smooth 1D manifold $\Gamma(s)$ tangent to $\mathbf{v}_0$ at $s=0$. Taking the inner product of $\Delta(s)\mathbf{1}_M = \Delta^\star\mathbf{1}_M + s^2(J\mathbf{w} + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{h}(\mathbf{v}_0)) + \mathcal{O}(s^3)$ with $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star$ eliminates $J\mathbf{w}$, establishing Eq.~\eqref{eq:kkt_curvature}. Finally, if $(\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}, \delta\boldsymbol{\lambda}, \delta\Delta)$ lies in the nullspace of the bordered matrix in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bordered_kkt}, left-multiplying $J\delta\boldsymbol{\theta} - \delta\Delta\mathbf{1}_M = \mathbf{0}$ by $\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\star\top}$ gives $-\delta\Delta = 0$ and $\delta\boldsymbol{\theta} = \alpha\mathbf{v}_0$; left-multiplying $H_{\mathcal{L}}\delta\boldsymbol{\theta} + J^\top\delta\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \mathbf{0}$ by $\mathbf{v}_0^\top$ gives $-2c\alpha = 0 \implies \delta\boldsymbol{\theta} = \mathbf{0}$, so $\delta\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \beta\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\star$ and $\mathbf{1}_M^\top\delta\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \beta = 0 \implies \delta\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \mathbf{0}$.
\end{proof}

\end{equation}
certified across all $2P-1 = 5,119$ shifts via Kronecker polynomial squaring in \texttt{fractions.Fraction} at $10^{16}$ and $10^{50}$. Within the $P \le 2,560$ single-step-function regime, this improves upon Matolcsi \& Vinuesa 2010 ($1.5098$) by $-0.005977$ and AlphaEvolve v1 2025 ($1.5053$) by $-0.001477$ (whereas recent variable-interval / $P \ge 30,000$ continuous and step constructions reach $1.50314$ in AlphaEvolve v2 \cite{alphaevolve2_2025}, $1.502870$ in \cite{khrulkov2026}, and $1.50286$ in TTT-Discover \cite{tttdiscover2026}).
\item \textbf{Second Autocorrelation Inequality ($C_2$, Cartridge 7):} Maximizing over nonnegative step weights $a_i \ge 0$ (\texttt{verify\_extremal\_cartridges\_7\_8.py:130}) \cite{jaech2025}:
\begin{equation}
C_2(a) = \frac{\|f*f\|_2^2}{\|f*f\|_1 \|f*f\|_\infty} = \frac{2\sum_{k=0}^{2P-2} g_k^2 + 2\sum_{k=0}^{2P-3} g_k g_{k+1}}{3\bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{2P-2} g_k\bigr)\max_{0 \le k \le 2P-2} g_k} \ge \mathbf{0.942158772814} \quad (P = 20,256,\; a_i \ge 0),
\tag{21}\label{eq:c2_bound}
\end{equation}
and $C_2 \ge \mathbf{0.942111374293}$ at $P = 10,128$. Near the optimum at $P = 20,256$, $\mathbf{50.93\%}$ ($\mathbf{20,632 / 40,511}$) of convolution nodes lie on a flat roof $g_k \ge 0.95 g_{\max}$, where single-index \texttt{argmax} subgradients oscillate across competing peaks. Our \textbf{Boltzmann Roof-Bundle Subgradient} replaces $\max_k g_k$ with $\widehat{g}_{\max}(\tau) = \sum_k \operatorname{stop\_grad}(w_k(\tau)) g_k$, $w_k(\tau) = \operatorname{softmax}((g_k - g_{\max})/\tau)$ annealed over $\tau \in [8\times 10^{-6}, 2\times 10^{-5}] g_{\max}$, surpassing Matolcsi \& Vinuesa ($0.88922$), AlphaEvolve v1 ($0.90530$), and Jaech \& Joseph (2025, $0.94136$ at $P=2399$) by $\mathbf{+0.00079877}$ within $P \le 20,256$ (complementary to $P \ge 50,000$ searches in TTT-Discover \cite{tttdiscover2026} $0.9595$ and AlphaEvolve v2 \cite{alphaevolve2_2025} $0.9610$).
\item \textbf{Third Autocorrelation Inequality for Signed Functions ($\Phi_3$, Problem 4 of \emph{Dream-RSI}):} Minimizing $\Phi_3(a) = \max_k |g_k|$ where $g_k = \frac{2}{P}(a*a)_k$ subject to $\frac{1}{P}\sum_i a_i = 1$ ($a_i \in \mathbb{R}$) \cite{cilleruelo2010}. Under the smooth bidirectional LogSumExp potential $L_\beta(a) = \frac{1}{\beta}\log \sum_k (\exp(\beta g_k) + \exp(-\beta g_k))$ with partition function $Z(\beta) = \sum_k (\exp(\beta g_k) + \exp(-\beta g_k))$, let $u_k(\beta) = \frac{\exp(\beta g_k) + \exp(-\beta g_k)}{Z(\beta)} \ge 0$ ($\sum_k u_k = 1$) and $w_k(\beta) = \frac{\exp(\beta g_k) - \exp(-\beta g_k)}{Z(\beta)} \in [-u_k, u_k]$. Dropping the indefinite Hankel second-derivative term yields an analytical \textbf{PSD Gauss-Newton preconditioner}:
\begin{equation}
H_{\text{GN}}(a) = \beta J(a)^\top \bigl(\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{u}(\beta)) - \mathbf{w}(\beta)\mathbf{w}(\beta)^\top\bigr) J(a) \succeq 0, \qquad J_{k,i}(a) = \frac{\partial g_k}{\partial a_i} = \frac{4}{P} a_{k-i}, \qquad \Phi_3 \le \mathbf{1.457058511867} \quad (P = 3,200),
\tag{22}\label{eq:phi3_bound}
\end{equation}
outperforming OpenEvolve ($1.4600$), EvoX ($1.4589$), Cilleruelo--Ruzsa--Vinuesa ($1.4581$), and ShinkaEvolve ($1.4578$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations, Failure Modes \& Broader Impact}

In accordance with rigorous peer-review standards, we explicitly document the limitations and boundary conditions of \texttt{SLEEP-DAG-RSI v8}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cold-Start Discovery Compute (Stage A) vs.\ Replay Verification Time (Stage B):}
While our verification and replay scripts (\texttt{auto\_dream\_rsi.py}, \texttt{verify\_extremal\_cartridges\_7\_8.py}, \texttt{heilbronn\_pr\_package/verify\_pr\_package.py}) execute in $0.4\text{--}3.9\text{ s}$, they operate over persisted intermediate checkpoints produced during Stage A cold-start search (Section~\ref{sec:stage_ab}, Table~\ref{tab:stage_ab}). Discovering the optimal topological chambers for Heilbronn $n=13\dots 17$ from random \texttt{std::mt19937\_64} seeds or polishing $P=20,256$ step convolutions via \texttt{Boltzmann Roof-Bundle Subgradient} requires $0.8\text{--}18.0$ CPU-hours on an AMD Ryzen 9 / 16-thread workstation.
\item \textbf{Expressivity Boundaries of \texttt{NeuroSymbolicDistiller} \& \texttt{SymbolicLibrary}:}
Our hierarchical distiller uses a strictly bounded grammar ($d_{\text{active}} \le 8$ gradient-selected inputs, $K \le 64$ basis atoms per expert) and relies on smooth 5-step Gauss-Newton refinement (\texttt{\_discover\_nonlinear\_inner\_submotif}) to crack inner arguments of saturating links $\tanh(\cdot)$. Laws involving deeply nested non-differentiable combinatorics (e.g., bitwise hashes, modulo arithmetic, or recursive data structures) cannot be extracted via sparse coordinate-descent Lasso and remain inside the residual neural weights $\Delta f_m^{\text{rem}}$ or require discrete LLM code synthesis.
\item \textbf{Diagnostic Richness Requirement \& Breakdown Point in \texttt{SDPO} (\texttt{FeedbackConditionedSelfTeacher}):}
Reclaiming $100\%$-dead-end batches in NREM sleep requires the verifier to return a quantitative residual vector or directional correction $\Delta_{\text{verifier}}(\mathbf{x}_i, f_i) \ne \mathbf{0}$. When an external black-box evaluator returns only a binary crash bit ($0/1$) with zero stacktrace or margin signal, \texttt{FeedbackConditionedSelfTeacher} abstains ($\omega_i^{\text{SDPO}} = 0$ by the zero-residual gate, holding $\Delta B_m^{\text{slow}} = \mathbf{0}$) and falls back to contrastive \texttt{DAG-DPO} repulsion. Furthermore, because the coordinate-wise median / MAD estimator has a theoretical finite-sample breakdown point of $\rho^\star = 50\%$, systematic adversarial bias exceeding $50\%$ of dead-end samples overwhelms the IRLS gate; in this regime, our validation-anchor fallback gate ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{val}}$) detects the non-decreasing anchor loss and reverts the update to prevent catastrophic corruption (Table~\ref{tab:trial6_ablations}, Trial 6B).
\item \textbf{High Algebraic Degree in Extremal Geometry ($\deg P(\Delta_n^\star) > 78$ at $n=13, 14$):}
Unlike $n=15$ (whose $D_3$-symmetric $12+3$ frame reduces via resultant elimination to a degree-5 minimal polynomial), LLL lattice reduction (\texttt{fmpz\_mat.lll} in \texttt{python-flint}) at 4,800-digit precision proves that $\Delta_{13}^\star$ and $\Delta_{14}^\star$ have no minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$ of degree $d \le 78$ with coefficient height $\le 10^{60}$. Consequently, for $n=13, 14, 16, 17$, our 500-digit KKT certificates and $10^{100}$-denominator exact rational floor embeddings certify exact KKT stationarity ($\Delta_n^\star$) and rigorous rational lower bounds ($\underline{\Delta}_n$) on the true geometric optima, rather than closed-form radical expressions.
\item \textbf{Broader Impact \& Trustworthy Autonomous Discovery:}
Unconstrained self-improving agents pose risks of \emph{Grader Gaming} (fabricating intermediate branches or exploiting floating-point inaccuracies) and unsafe code execution. \texttt{SLEEP-DAG-RSI v8} mitigates these risks structurally via: (i) \texttt{AstPolicySafetyGate} (static AST whitelisting blocking \texttt{eval}, \texttt{exec}, \texttt{import}, dunder access, and filesystem/network calls); (ii) mandatory inverse projection $\Phi^{-1}$ into the base domain prior to external evaluation; (iii) the pre-committed hypothesis contract $\mathcal{H} = (c, \pi, d, a)$; and (iv) two-layer formal verification in \texttt{Lean 4} (\texttt{by native\_decide}, zero \texttt{Float}, zero \texttt{sorryAx}).
\end{enumerate}
